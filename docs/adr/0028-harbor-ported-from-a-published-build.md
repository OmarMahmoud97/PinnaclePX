# Harbor, ported from a published build: PrebuiltUI's Forged as t06

- Status: accepted
- Date: 16 September 2026
- Builds on: ADR 0008 (the template contract), ADR 0023 (the three ports and their rules), ADR 0027 (Ember, the first port from a published build), `docs/template-porting-guide.md`

## Context

The owner asked for PrebuiltUI's gym template, Forged, to become `t06`, built exactly as it is. As with Restro (ADR 0027), the repository folder (prebuiltui/prebuiltui, `templates/forged-gym-website-template`, MIT, copyright 2025 PrebuiltUI) has only ever held a README, and the template's published build at forged.prebuiltui.com is the source: a server-rendered Next.js site whose HTML carries every element and class, whose stylesheet carries its theme, its two fonts and its own utilities, and whose script chunk carries all thirteen blocks as client components, with their motion configuration, their data (the FAQ answers, the count-up targets, the partner names) and the contact form's submission.

The port proceeds under the owner's confirmation of 15 September 2026 that PrebuiltUI's templates are cleared for use in a product that is sold, given for Restro and taken to cover this template from the same publisher and repository. If that is not the owner's intent, this port waits.

## Decision

1. **The build is the source, read whole.** The HTML, the stylesheet (Tailwind 4; `--font-body` Inter and `--font-heading` Space Grotesk, both variable; a `:root` palette of near-black `#0b0b0b`, lime `#d7ff2f` and a few greys; `container-gym` at 1440px with 2rem, 4rem and 6rem of padding; `section-spacing` at 7.5rem; a 25s marquee; a lime scrollbar and selection) and the script chunk (every block's markup and props, framer-motion's `FadeUp` at 40px over 0.6s, `SlideIn` at 60px over 0.7s, the hero's timed entrances, the bar's scroll listener at forty pixels, the count-up over 2.2s, the FAQ's state, the form's post to `api.formsync.app` under the id `your-form-id` with a toast) were saved, pretty-printed and read in full. Nothing was taken from a screenshot or from memory.
2. **Every block is ported, class for class, with the tree the source has.** Thirteen blocks in the source's order: the bar and its phone overlay, the hero, About, the capabilities, the figures, the gallery, the plans, the testimonials, the partners, the FAQ and form, the closing band, the articles and the footer. The source is Tailwind 4, so every utility is kept verbatim (`h-8.5`, `text-[18vw]`, `text-on-surface/2.5`, `tracking-[0.25em]`, `space-y-3`); only `container-gym` and `section-spacing` are written out and the eighty inline `font-family: Space Grotesk` declarations become the `font-display` class, since a template cannot name its faces.
3. **The token map for a dark palette of tints.** The source paints nearly everything as white or lime at an alpha, which maps one for one: `#0b0b0b` is `surface`, `#0f0f0f` `surface-muted`, `#111` `accent`, `#1a1a1a` `border` (as a border and as a fill), `#d7ff2f` `brand-deeper`, `#c8f020` `brand-deepest`, near-black on lime `on-brand`, white `on-surface`, and every `white/60`, `white/40`, `#d7ff2f/10` or `#0b0b0b/70` the same alpha on the token. Its three other greys are white at an alpha: `#222` is `on-surface/10`, `#333` `on-surface/16`, `#555` `on-surface/30`. Its lime glow shadows are the same shadows through `color-mix` on `brand-deeper`; its `#1a2000` hover on the lit card is eight percent of the accent in the surface. Its footer, three units darker than the page (`#080808`), is the page.
4. **Nine pieces are optional, not absent.** About's badge and its two quotes, the gallery, the plans, the testimonials, the partners, the articles, the social links and the newsletter's address need facts the brief does not hold, so they are `| null`, never written by the copy stage, and filled on the example from the source's own copy and its Unsplash photographs. The three figures in the hero and the grid of figures stay, as short phrases with no numbers on a visitor's page (as Monolith's highlights do); the contact form and the footer's email field post to the owner's address as a mail message when it is known and otherwise lead to the closing band; the main buttons lead to the contact form, since the source's led to plans a visitor's page does not have. Three image slots: the hero's photograph, About's and the closing band's.
5. **Motion plays as the source's did, and the page is complete without it.** The bar drops in and the hero's parts rise on a timer at the source's travels, lengths, waits and curves (the headline's three lines out of clipped rows on the site's own expo curve, which is the source's `[0.16, 1, 0.3, 1]`; its wrappers' `[0.25, 0.1, 0.25, 1]`, which is CSS `ease`; its library's default for a plain tween, which is `ease-out`). Every block below plays its entrance once, the first time it comes into view: a small observer (`sections/reveal.tsx`, the source's `useInView` in twenty lines) marks a block shown when it enters at the margin the block names (80px in for the source's FadeUp and SlideIn wrappers, 50px for the capability cards, 40px for the cells, testimonials and articles, none for the rest), and the stylesheet plays the keyframe at the block's length, wait and curve, then leaves it alone so a later hover is not pinned. Blocks are hidden only under `@media (scripting: enabled)` and with motion allowed, so a page without a script, or a reader who asked for less motion, sees everything at once. The first port of this template used scroll-driven `view()` keyframes instead, which scrub with the scroll rather than play, and do nothing in a browser without `animation-timeline`; the owner saw the entrances as missing on 16 September 2026 and they were rebuilt this way. Each figure in the grid counts up from zero over the source's 2.2s when it comes into view (`sections/count.tsx`), ending on the value as written; a phrase stands. The bar turns to glass forty pixels down by the source's own scroll listener; the phone overlay is state, its parts arriving by starting-style transitions at the source's 80ms stagger and the whole fading out over the source's 250ms before it goes; Escape closes it. The marquee is the source's own keyframe; buttons swell under the pointer and shrink under the finger with `scale`, a plan lifts on `transform` so the entrance keeps `translate`. The FAQ rows are native disclosure elements, their open colours as the source's state set them and their hover written under `:not([open])`, because a hover variant outranks an open variant while the pointer rests on the row it has just opened.

## Consequences

- Six templates are ready; the selector chooses three of six per submission. The registry test's "template without a contract" case moved from `t06-harbor` to `t07-summit`.
- Verified on 16 September 2026 against the published build: every one of the thirteen blocks matches the source's rendered height to the pixel at 1535px and at 390px, and the screenshot pairs match (the source's pairs show elements mid-entrance, since its motion library ignores reduced motion). At 1920 and 390 in both schemes there is no horizontal overflow and no console error. With motion allowed: the hero plays on load, a block below the fold is hidden until it comes into view and then plays once, the figures count up to their written values, the bar is clear at the top and glass after forty pixels, and the overlay fades out over 250ms before it goes; with JavaScript off, and under reduced motion, every block is visible at once. The marquee runs, an open question takes the accent, a plan lifts eight pixels, the lit card takes its lime-black hover, and the overlay's one close mark closes it by tap, by Enter and by Escape.
- axe (WCAG 2.2 AA tags) reports one rule, colour contrast, on the source's own design: it sets its secondary text at 30 to 60 percent white on near-black (labels, descriptions, the partners' names, the small print) and at 60 to 70 percent near-black on lime, none of which reaches 4.5:1; 41 such nodes at 1920 on the example. Because those are alphas on the template's own text token rather than a token the solver can move, a derived set inherits them (65 nodes on the derived light set). Kept as the source has them, since the owner asks for the source's look; raising the alphas is the owner's call and is listed in `docs/claims-register.md` beside the other inherited findings.
- Deviations from the source, all deliberate: the hero's three `h1` elements are one `h1` over three clipped rows; the bar's links sit in a `nav` and its buttons are labelled; the form's labels are tied to their fields and the newsletter's field and button are labelled; the logo links to the top of the page, not to `/`; each plan's `button`, which did nothing, is a link to the contact form; the form's success state and toasts are not ported; the source showed two close marks while the overlay was open (its bar's toggle, above the overlay, and the overlay's own), and here the toggle hides while the overlay is open and its close mark takes focus, at the owner's request on 16 September 2026; the document's 4px lime scrollbar is not ported (a template cannot style the document); the source's `Learn More` and the maker's line in the footer are slots; the unnamed blocks carry ids (`metrics`, `gallery`, `testimonials`, `partners`, `cta`, `blog`) so links and the comparison can name them.
- `docs/claims-register.md` row "A person designs every layout" still needs the owner's decision: five of the six ready layouts were designed by other people and adapted here.

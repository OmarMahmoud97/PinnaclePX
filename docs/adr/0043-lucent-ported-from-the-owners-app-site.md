# Lucent, ported from the owner's own app site as t10

- Status: accepted, built and verified on 1 October 2026; registered but not ready (out of rotation) until the explicit visit cap the owner set is built
- Date: 1 October 2026
- Amends: ADR 0041 (the set is ten, the ninth and tenth not ready), ADR 0038 (its implicit two-visit cap)
- Builds on: ADR 0008 (the template contract), ADR 0023 (the ports and their rules), ADR 0041 (porting a live site with hand-written CSS), `docs/template-porting-guide.md`

## Context

The owner, 1 October 2026: "I would like you to build one last website template, I will provide you the code for the website. I need you to build it as good as possible as a template. ensuring it is perfect on desktop, tablet and mobile. you must also ensure all of the animation is pefect and aligns with the code I made. be thorough and concise. it is essential you do not hallucinate, guess or assume in this process." Asked whether the code was theirs, which slot it took and how the visit cap should change: "yep you can build it", "yes slot 10 please - give it a nice name", and "it will now be 4 visits and 3 designs a visit as there are now 10 templates".

The pasted code was the rendered DOM of subscrr.app, the site of the owner's own iPhone app, and it was cut off at the paste limit partway through the pricing, with no stylesheet and no script. With the owner's go-ahead, the live site's source was downloaded instead: its server HTML, `styles.css`, `script.js`, `nav-glass.js`, `theme.js`, `rise-blur.js`, `newsletter.js`, `analytics.js`, its two vendor GSAP files and every asset the page references. Named Lucent: the page is glass and clarity, "one honest number".

## Decision

1. **The live page is the source, read whole and run.** Every file above was read to its end and the page was served locally beside the port. Every claim below about the source's behaviour was measured in a browser on that copy: boxes, colours and frame-by-frame motion.
2. **A scoped stylesheet with the source's numbers.** The source is hand-written CSS in pixels, so its rules are ported one for one into `lucent.css` under `.lucent`, every class prefixed `lc-` so none can meet the site's. Two cascade facts of the source are kept because they decide what it renders: its rule for every heading with a class (`h2[class]`, an element and a class) outranks single-class rules, so the feature cards' titles render at 500 rather than the 900 their own rule asks, and the form's heading in Inter at 500 rather than Inter Tight at 600; and its general margin for the email form comes after the desktop block in its file, so on a desktop the form sits 4% of the screen's height below the picture's middle. Its form controls keep the browser's own line height and letter spacing, which the site's reset would otherwise replace.
3. **Sixteen blocks in the source's order, mapped to a business's words.** The glass bar; the hero with its phone; the wide film; the overview; the white card of three short lines; the reminder with its notice; the three steps (the source's import); the rail of four feature cards; the wide picture under the tint (the source's widgets); the pair of tall pictures (its watch); the statement; the pricing; the questions; the promise card (its privacy card); the picture card with its email form (its journal); the dark footer with its big ask. Optional, because the brief holds no such facts: the pricing, a feature card's badge (the source's "Beta") and the footer's networks; the copy stage leaves them null and the page is complete without them. Fourteen image slots, one per picture or film. The nav's four links follow the page by position (the overview, the rail's last card, the questions, the ask), every button leads to the ask, and the ask is the footer, whose big line and white pill open a mail message to the owner, or lead to the email form when there is no address.
4. **The token map.** The source's warm paper is `surface` and its second paper `surface-muted`, its white cards `accent`, its ink and grey `on-surface` and `on-surface-muted`, its orange `brand` and `brand-deeper` with white as `on-brand`, its peach and periwinkle the two glows, its near-black footer the `scrim` with `on-scrim`. Its other colours are a palette derived from the tokens, which the example sets to the source's own values through `--lucent-set-*`: the periwinkle of its small capitals (the muted ink by default), the black of its one dark card (the scrim), the peach of the page's tint (the glow mixed into the surface), pure white for its glass (the scrim's text), its dimmer body text, its lines, its footer grey, its plan's wash and, in its night theme, its grain, its bar's glass and its feature tiles (decision 6). The brand's colour where it sits on the near-black (the footer's ask, the dark card's heading and link) is not a contrast pair: one colour cannot be solved against both the light page and the dark scrim, and the pipeline solves one set over every chosen template's pairs (`lib/inngest/functions/build-concepts.ts`), so declaring it made the solver give up for every brand. It is lifted on the dark to at least 0.66 OKLCH lightness where the browser can turn a colour (about five to one on the scrim), and is the scrim's text colour elsewhere. `tests/integration/template-pairs.test.ts` now proves that every template's pairs, alone and all ten together, settle at AA for the adversarial corpus in both schemes.
5. **Motion as the source plays it, measured against it.** The source ran its entrances and scrubs on GSAP 3.12 with ScrollTrigger and the rest on the Web Animations API; templates may not import GSAP, so every piece is written on the Web Animations API or frame by frame, with GSAP's power curves as sampled `linear()` easings (power2.out is cubic, power3.out quartic, power4.out quintic) and its scrub as an expo.out chase restarted at every scroll step.
   - The splash: the name's letters rise from 12.15% below out of a 14px blur, 0.6s each on power2.out, 0.04s apart, at twice that speed on a desktop and three times on a phone or touch screen; the sheet fades over 0.4s (0.3s on a phone) once the last has risen, or after 1.8s whatever happens; under reduced motion the name stands for 0.7s. The source drew its wordmark; here the name is set in the heading face, at the size that gives a name of its seven letters the drawing's width (measured equal at 1535, 1024, 768 and 390px).
   - The hero, once the fonts are in and the splash has begun to fade: the headline's lines slide up out of their clips (0.62s, 0.07s apart), the phone fades in out of a 10px blur (1.1s after 0.3s), the lead and the buttons rise 14px out of a 10px blur (1.1s, 0.18s and 0.3s after the last line starts); the headline's own words return at the first resize after, as the source restored them then. Measured by wrapping `Element.animate` on both pages: every animation is created within a millisecond of the sheet's fade and starts on the next frame, with the same delays.
   - Every small line, paragraph and picture rises 40px out of nothing over 1s on power4.out when its top, as it stands, reaches 90% of the screen (ScrollTrigger measured the moved box: with the layout top past the line but the moved top short of it, the source did not play); the steps' pills clear out of a 10px blur (0.9s on power3.out, 0.12s apart, at 88%); the statement's words rise out of their clips (0.9s on power4.out, 0.02s apart, at 82%). The plans clear out of a 14px blur (1.1s on power3.out, 0.14s apart, at 85%), written frame by frame into the cards' style as GSAP wrote them, because the source's cards ease every change of transform over half a second on a spring, so their rise trails their fade; a Web Animation would have skipped that.
   - The section headings' lines rise out of a mask (0.8s, 0.1s apart) when the heading crosses 88% of the screen; the white card's lines and the picture card's caption rise out of a 10px blur (0.9s, 0.12s apart); the footer's ask slides up line by line (0.8s, 0.1s apart); the notice drops onto the reminder's picture when a third of the card is on the screen. The lines are built over the heading rather than in it, so nothing React drew is touched and readers keep the words.
   - Scrubbed with the scroll: the overview's and the white card's pictures drift from 7% up to 7% down at 112%, the reminder's picture from 6% up to 6% down at 110% while the notice drifts from 42% to 58% of the card, the promo's picture grows to 116%, and its frame grows to 120% between its section reaching the screen's foot and reaching 18% of it, chasing the scroll with a 0.5s lag. Measured at six scroll positions on both pages: equal to four significant figures. The lines are measured from each block's box as ScrollTrigger measured them (a picture inside the white card's border was 2px out when summed from offsets).
   - The tint warms the whole page around the wide picture: over 85% of a screen in and out on a desktop, and on a phone or tablet from when the hero's phone has gone 5% of a screen above the top, out as the block's foot passes half a screen. The bar's tint thickens after 30px; the button back to the top shows after a screen. The bar's lens is the source's liquid glass, its maps and its 22-step filter ported verbatim, not in Firefox nor on a small screen, stopping 14px short of the ask.
   - The buttons follow the pointer by three tenths of its distance from their centre; the questions ease open (0.4s) and shut (0.3s); the pricing switch's thumb springs to the chosen option in the press itself and the price rolls over 0.5s on a cubic; the rail's dots follow its snapping scroll and a dot moves it; the dark card's heading is a cloud of dots that gathers at a tap, the source's physics ported verbatim (measured: 607 by 147 and gathered in about 755ms on both pages).
   - The page scrolls natively, as the source's did (its own script turned its Lenis off): the root carries `data-scroll-native`, which the site's smooth scroll now honours by leaving the wheel and the page's own links to the browser.
6. **Deviations, all deliberate.** The source's films are pictures here, since the pipeline has no film: the hero's phone shows the hero picture under the source's own frame, tinted to the brand (its metal turned grey, the brand's hue laid over it) unless the page asks for the source's finish, as the example does; the feature windows and the framed tile take a picture each; the promo's sound button goes with the sound. The hero's QR card is not ported: it led to the App Store, and a business has no code to scan. The first hero button's Apple logo is an envelope, since the ask is a mail message, and the footer's App Store badge is a white pill of the brand's words at the badge's height. Not ported, as a business page cannot have them: the theme switch and the clock that turned the page dark from 20:00 to 05:00 (a brand's scheme comes from its tokens), the cookie consent and the cookie links, the analytics, the newsletter's endpoint (the form mails the owner, ADR 0008). The source's WebGL mesh gradient is not ported: its own stylesheet hid it (`#gl { display: none }`) while its script drew it every frame. The source's night theme lifted each feature picture into a tile with rounder corners because its films were shot on white; a photograph needs no such help, so a brand's page keeps the picture running past the card's foot, and the example's dark scheme asks for the tiles (`--lucent-set-tiles`). The particle cloud rests while its card is off the screen (the source drew it every frame) and gathers when focus arrives in its card's text as well as at a tap, so a keyboard visitor gets the heading. A link in the page moves focus to its target as the browser's jump would. Under reduced motion the rail's dots and the button to the top jump rather than glide. The notice that drops onto the reminder's picture was a picture in the source; here it is drawn to the picture's measurements, frosted over whatever picture is behind it, with its time pinned top right as a phone sets it so its line runs on beneath; its line is held to 40 characters, which fill it in the body face. The source's films were stills taken at the frame its page shows and cropped to its windows; the source showed its phone films larger on a tablet, which one crop cannot match. The example uses the source's own name, words and pictures rather than Kestrel: the source is the owner's own product, its pictures show the app by name, and the owner compares the two pages line by line.
7. **Every screen.** The owner asked for desktop, tablet and phone to be perfect. At rest the port matches the source to the pixel at 1535, 1024, 768 and 390px (decision 8); beyond those, two of the source's faults are fixed at the sizes where they show and nowhere else: below 360px the headings shrink with the screen (the source's 50px ran its hero's longest word past a 320px screen), and the single-column grids are `minmax(0, 1fr)`, so a brand's word too long for its column breaks inside it rather than widening the page.
8. **Verification**, 1 October 2026, against the source served locally and the port on the dev server and a production build:
   - Layout at rest (reduced motion): every element with a class compared box for box at 1535, 1024, 768 and 390px, light and dark. 186 of 190 boxes agree within a pixel at 1535px; the four that do not are the splash name's height (its width agrees), the switch's thumb (the source measures it before its fonts arrive and never again: 97px where the option is 100px), the footer's pill and the footer's links (decision 6). The text of every line agrees in place, size and line count, but for the footer's links. Section heights at 1535px agree to the pixel.
   - Colour: every element's text, background and border colour agrees within two units in both schemes, but for placeholders and pictures.
   - Motion: the frame-by-frame samples above, on both pages through the same action; where the source's frames lagged it was the cost of its hidden canvas, not its curve.
   - Responsiveness: 41 sizes from 320 by 640 to 2560 by 1440, with four short landscape screens; nothing scrolls sideways, sits past the screen's edge or spills from its box, and the hero's headline clears the bar at every size.
   - Cost, on a production build, the same scripted load and person-paced wheel scroll, three runs each: script time over the scroll 81 to 87ms against the source's 940 to 1,318ms; all main-thread tasks 1,183 to 1,295ms against 1,883 to 2,500ms; the load to rest 365 to 464ms against 1,998 to 2,500ms; at most two frames over 34ms against up to 22.
   - axe (WCAG 2.1 AA and best practice) finds only contrast, and on the example only the source's own colours: its grey on its paper (4.02:1), white on its orange and its orange on white (3.8:1), and its badges' orange on their tints (3.07 and 2.44:1). In a brand's derived colours (the example's `?scheme=brand` and `?scheme=brand-dark`) the only findings are the two badges, which a brand's page never shows. The console is clean in every scheme.
   - `pnpm test`, `pnpm lint`, `pnpm typecheck`, `pnpm knip`, `pnpm format:check` and `pnpm build` pass, and the bundle budgets hold.

## Consequences

- Ten templates are registered (`TemplateTuple`, `CONFIG.templates.count`); Inegro and Lucent are `ready: false`, so the selector still chooses from ADR 0038's eight and a returning email still meets its implicit two-visit cap.
- The owner set the cap at four visits of three designs. With ten ready templates a fourth visit would find one unseen design, and the selector builds nothing when fewer than three remain (`lib/select/select.ts`), so four full visits need twelve templates, or a fourth visit of fewer designs. The explicit cap, and with it `ready: true` for Inegro and Lucent, waits on the owner's answer; it also needs both templates' poster descriptors (`lib/preview/descriptors.ts`), the copy that promises a return visit (`app/_components/straight-answer-items.ts`) and the exhausted state's words (`app/start/_components/done-copy.ts`).
- The example page has four schemes: the source by day (default), by night (`?scheme=dark`), and a brand's derived colours and type, light and dark (`?scheme=brand`, `?scheme=brand-dark`).
- The site's smooth scroll honours `data-scroll-native` on a page's root (`app/_components/smooth-scroll.tsx`).
- Owner decisions, each standing on the default above until the owner says otherwise: (1) the visit cap and readiness, above; (2) whether a business's page should carry the source's phone at all, since its frame is an iPhone's; (3) whether the hero's first button should keep an icon, now an envelope; (4) whether the footer should keep the source's "Contact us" that copies the owner's address.

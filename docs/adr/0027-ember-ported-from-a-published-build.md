# Ember, ported from a published build: PrebuiltUI's Restro as t05

- Status: accepted
- Date: 15 September 2026
- Builds on: ADR 0008 (the template contract), ADR 0023 (the three ports and their rules), `docs/template-porting-guide.md`

## Context

The owner asked for PrebuiltUI's restaurant template, Restro, to become `t05`, built "100% as it is currently built within GitHub". The repository (prebuiltui/prebuiltui, MIT, copyright 2025 PrebuiltUI) holds only a README for it, and has only ever held that README: every template folder in that repository is a README with a screenshot and two links, and the code is distributed from prebuiltui.com. What is public is the template's published build at restro.prebuiltui.com, a server-rendered Next.js site whose HTML carries every element and every Tailwind class, whose stylesheet carries its theme, its fonts and its one custom utility, whose script chunks carry its client components and their data, and whose server-component payload carries the props of every animated wrapper.

The owner chose to port from that build rather than from the HTML alone, and confirmed the template is cleared for use in a product that is sold. Both are recorded here and in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`.

## Decision

1. **The build is the source, read whole.** The page's HTML was saved and pretty-printed; the stylesheet read in full (Tailwind 4, its `orange-500` and `orange-600` overridden to `#fe6e00` and `#f05100`, its body at the small text size in black on white, Geist on the page and Urbanist on the two big headlines and the footer's watermark, and `px-auto`, a utility of its own: 1.5rem of horizontal padding, 3rem from md, 6rem from lg, 10rem from xl); the script chunks pretty-printed and read for the header (a scroll listener at ten pixels, a sheet toggled by state), the dishes (a half-turn on a spring under the pointer), the smooth-scroll instance (Lenis, 1.2s, anchors offset by 120px) and the `Animated` wrapper (a spring of stiffness 320, damping 70 and mass 1, once, from 50px below by default); and the server-component payload decoded for the sixty-nine wrappers' directions and delays. Nothing was taken from a screenshot or from memory.
2. **Every block is ported, class for class, with the tree the source has.** Twelve blocks in the source's order: the header and its sheet, the hero, About, the numbered points, the dishes, the features, the booking steps, the opening times, the testimonials, the FAQ, the closing band and the footer. The animated wrappers stay as wrapping `div`s carrying `data-rise` (the hero, on a timer) or `data-fade` (everything else, once in view) and the source's delay and travel. The source is Tailwind 4, as this project is, so its `space-*` and every spacing utility are kept verbatim; only `px-auto` is written out, and the two arbitrary background-image utilities that named its pictures become inline backgrounds from the image slots.
3. **The token map for a fixed light palette with many greys.** White is `surface`; slate-50 (the hover wash and the portrait rings) `surface-muted`; black `on-surface`; zinc-600, the body grey, `on-surface-muted`; slate-200 `border`; the two oranges `brand-deeper` and `brand-deepest`; white on orange `on-brand`. The source's other four greys are the same black at an alpha, which lands within two units of each on white and inverts correctly on a dark set: zinc-800 is `on-surface/85`, zinc-700 `/75`, zinc-500 `/55`, zinc-400 `/37`; its slate-300 ring is `on-surface/20`, its `bg-black/5` disc `on-surface/5`, and its black-on-white and white-on-black pills `on-surface` on `surface` and the reverse. The two laurel marks beside About's eyebrow are the source's own black PNGs, drawn as masks over `on-surface` so a brand's set recolours them.
4. **Six pieces are optional, not absent.** The hero's rating line and guests, the location card, the booking testimonial, the opening times, the testimonials and the social links need facts the brief does not hold, so they are `| null`, never written by the copy stage, and filled on the example from the source's own copy. The card of opening times carries a sentence in place of its rows when they are null, then its button as before; the dishes' prices are short notes; the footer's phone is null and its mail link is the owner's address from `TemplateAssets.email`. Twelve image slots: the hero's photograph, About's, the features' portrait, the opening times' photograph and the eight dishes; the closing band's corner pictures are the first four dishes'.
5. **Motion plays as the source's did, and the page is complete without it.** The source's wrapper is one spring for everything (stiffness 320, damping 70, mass 1: over critical damping, so no overshoot), played once, the first time any part of a wrapper came into view, with no margin, after the wrapper's own delay. Run to rest the way the library runs it, the spring settles in 1.18s for the longest travel (150px) and every travel follows the same curve (opacity, the slowest value on the others, rests at 1.09s), so every entrance here is one keyframe of 1.18s on that curve: the spring itself, sampled into a `linear()` easing at twenty points (within a hundredth of the true curve), with the closest cubic bezier as the fallback where `linear()` is not understood. The hero's parts are on screen at load and play on a timer. Every block below plays its entrance once, the first time it comes into view: a small observer (`sections/reveal.tsx`, the source's `whileInView` in twenty lines) marks a block shown when it enters, and the stylesheet plays the keyframe at the block's wait and travel with `animation-fill-mode: backwards`, then leaves it alone so a later hover is not pinned. Blocks are hidden only under `@media (scripting: enabled)` and with motion allowed, so a page without a script, or a reader who asked for less motion, sees everything at once. The first port used scroll-driven `view()` keyframes instead, which scrub with the scroll rather than play, and do nothing in a browser without `animation-timeline`; the owner saw the entrances as missing on 16 September 2026 and they were rebuilt this way, as Harbor's were (ADR 0028). The header turns to glass ten pixels down by the source's own scroll listener, over its 300ms transition. A dish turns half a turn each time the pointer reaches its cell and stays there, the turns adding up as the source's counter did (`sections/dish.tsx`); the turn is a transition on the source's second spring (stiffness 80, damping 15: under critical damping, 0.98s to rest, overshooting by a degree and a half), sampled the same way. The sheet is state, inert while closed and closed by Escape. The source's Lenis is not ported (templates cannot import it, and the site's own instance glides on previews); its 120px anchor offset is `scroll-mt-30` on every anchored block.

## Consequences

- Five templates are ready; the selector chooses three of five per submission. The registry test's "template without a contract" case moved from `t05-ember` to `t06-harbor`.
- Verified on 15 September 2026 against the published build: every one of the twelve blocks matches the source's rendered height to the pixel at 1535px and at 390px, and the screenshot pairs match (the source's pairs show elements mid-entrance, since its motion library ignores reduced motion). At 1920 and 390 in both schemes there is no horizontal overflow and no console error. axe (WCAG 2.2 AA tags) on the light example reports only the source's own colours: white on its orange (the buttons, the location card, the closing band) and its orange on white (the eyebrows, the step numbers) at 2.82:1, and its zinc-400 "Closed" at 37 percent; a derived set passes every declared pair, and its only finding is that same example-only "Closed" row, which is null on a visitor's page. Kept as the source has them.
- Motion verified on 16 September 2026 on the example, through Playwright, after the rebuild: the stylesheet's curve resolves to the sampled `linear()`; the hero's parts settle on load; a block below the fold is hidden and unmarked until it comes into view, is then marked and plays once (About's picture from four fifths, a feature row from 150px, over 1.18s), and every one of the 74 blocks below the hero (the source's 79 wrappers less the hero's five) is marked after a scroll through; the header is clear at the top and glass twenty pixels down; a dish's picture stands at rest, turns to 180 degrees on the first pointer entry and to 360 on the second, over 0.98s, and never turns back; under reduced motion, and with JavaScript off, every block is visible at once and nothing animates; at 1920 and 390 in both schemes there is no horizontal overflow and no console error.
- Deviations from the source, all deliberate: the header's logo links to the top of the page, not to prebuiltui.com; the eyebrow over the headline carries `uppercase` (the source typed its own in capitals); the closing band's headline is an `h2` (the source has two `h1`s) and the header's links sit in a `nav`; the menu buttons are labelled; the footer's "Designed by PrebuiltUI" is the photographers' credit line, and its "© 2026. All Right Reserved." is "© year, legal name. All rights reserved."; the corner pictures on the closing band have empty alt text, being repeats; the Instagram mark's path had a stray space in the source (`1. 145.333`), which stopped the browser drawing the rest of it and logged an error on every load, and is `1.145.333` here.
- `docs/claims-register.md` row "A person designs every layout" still needs the owner's decision: four of the five ready layouts were designed by other people and adapted here.
- The porting guide gains the recipe for a source that is only a published build.

# The hero sketch loops, and builds into a finished page

- Status: accepted
- Date: 3 September 2026
- Amends: 0005 (item 6, autoplay loops)

## Context

ADR 0005 allowed one authored moment in the hero, the sketch painting an example brief once, and listed autoplay loops among the things the page never does, on the evidence in `docs/home-page-design-research.md` section 4 (auto-forwarding content is ignored or resented, and WCAG 2.2.2 requires a pause control for anything that moves for more than five seconds beside other content). The owner has since asked for the moment to become a loop in which the visitor watches the website come together, from the wireframe to a finished page. The plan is `docs/hero-loop-plan.md`.

## Decision

1. **The hero loops, as an owner decision.** After the brief completes, the wireframe builds into a finished page, holds, clears back to the blank sketch, and the next sentence starts typing. The loop pauses while the hero is off screen, stops with `requestAnimationFrame` in a hidden tab, and is killed for good on the first keystroke in the sentence field. A loop cap is one number away in `CONFIG.demo` and has not been set.
2. **No visible pause control, at the owner's instruction (3 September 2026).** The Pause button from 0005 is removed as unwanted chrome. This is the one knowing departure from WCAG 2.2.2 (Level A), which asks for a mechanism to pause, stop or hide content that moves automatically for more than five seconds beside other content; the loop runs about 15 seconds a pass and does not end. The remaining mitigations are the three automatic ones above plus `prefers-reduced-motion`, none of which is the "mechanism" the criterion means. No automated check catches this: axe cannot detect it, so the accessibility suite stays green. Restoring conformance means either putting a control back or making the motion stop within five seconds; a loop cap does not do it, since three passes still run for about 45 seconds.
3. **The build is FLIP on GSAP core, hand-rolled.** Each sketch part and its counterpart on the finished page share a `data-part` name. At every build the code measures both and a pure helper (`lib/motion/flip.ts`) gives the inverse transform; finished parts tween from it to identity and sketch parts to the inverse, crossing in flight. Boxes scale, text only travels. The brief, the build with its hold, and the reset are three forward-only timelines chained by completion, never a repeating one, because a repeat rewinds every tween to its start values and fires every callback again; at the reset the build is stopped and everything it wrote is cleared by name, since a revert would also take back the fade in progress and `clearProps: 'all'` wipes React's inline colour variables too. No plugin joins the loader: eleven pairs and one rule did not earn Flip's bytes or its swap semantics.
4. **The photograph first paints small.** Chrome counts only an element's initial painted size for LCP, ignores opacity-0 paints, and never re-evaluates growth (web.dev/articles/lcp, read today). So the photograph enters as the example brief's own photo inside the sketch's image block at stage 4, at about half the H1's area at 1440 px, and the same picture in the finished layer swaps for it and travels. A Playwright test asserts the H1 is still the LCP element after a whole build with no input. Below `md` the phone frame sits under the button, outside the first screen; from `md` the browser frame is `max-w-xl`, narrower than on desktop, so the photograph's first painted area stays below the subhead's, and a tablet project asserts it (`docs/hero-loop-mobile-plan.md`).
5. **The finished page is client-only.** It loads through `next/dynamic` with `ssr: false` on a client that allows motion, so the server HTML does not grow and reduced-motion and JavaScript-off visitors, who keep the finished sketch exactly as before, download neither its chunk, nor its two font families (Montserrat and Caveat Brush via `next/font/google`, `preload: false`), nor the photograph. The photograph itself is a 640 by 480 WebP from Pexels under its commercial licence, no face, recorded with its provenance in `app/_components/photos.ts`; a supplied photograph replaces it by changing one import.
6. **Honesty.** The finished page is labelled the moment it appears, in the mono register: "The same example brief, built as an illustration. Not a client, and not one of the designs." Its words live in `app/_components/built-copy.ts`, its colours derive from the brief's one hex through relative oklch (`builtTintsFrom`), and its paragraph is the brief's own sentence.
7. **Numbers.** The loop's durations and the build's beats are `CONFIG.demo`. Measured after `next build` on 3 September 2026: initial scripts on `/` 206,202 B gzipped (was 201,002; budget 210,000), HTML 21,602 B (was 21,370), stylesheet 12,067 B (was 11,754). The stylesheet budget in `scripts/bundle-budget.mjs` and the design plan's section 8 moves from 12 KB to 14 KB rather than trimming unrelated CSS. GSAP stays a lazy chunk; the budget script still fails if it is not.

## Consequences

- 0005's rules otherwise stand: the H1 and subhead never animate, nothing outside the frame moves, tweens touch transform and opacity only (the photograph's saturation returning as it lands is a colour change), and the server renders the finished sketch.
- Every sketch part carries a `data-part` attribute on `/start` too, unused there; the frames gain a `built` slot and three stacked layers inside their page area.
- `sketch_replay` and `sketch_pause` are no longer analytics events: there is no finished state to replay from and no control to press. Nothing about the hero's motion is measurable from the visitor's side any more.
- A resize during a build finishes on the old measurements; the next loop measures afresh.
- If the field data shows long frames on phones during the build, the first thing to try is dropping the saturation tween, the only paint-heavy beat.

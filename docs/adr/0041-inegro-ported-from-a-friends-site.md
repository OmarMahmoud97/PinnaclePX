# Inegro, ported from a friend's WordPress site as t09

- Status: accepted, built and verified on 30 September 2026; registered but not ready (out of rotation) until an explicit visit cap replaces the one ADR 0038 derives from eight templates
- Date: 30 September 2026
- Amends: ADR 0038 (the set is nine, the ninth not ready)
- Replaces: the Intaglio build of 27 to 30 September 2026 (`t09-intaglio`, never committed), deleted at the owner's request on 30 September 2026
- Builds on: ADR 0008 (the template contract), ADR 0023 (the ports and their rules), ADR 0030 (porting from a published page), `docs/template-porting-guide.md`

## Context

The owner, 30 September 2026: "I would like to delete the intaglio template and I will provide you the code to a react project I would like you to build as a template instead", then, pasting the page: "my friend built this website for a client of his and said I can use it as a template", and "feel free to now build the template to the highest quality possible".

The pasted code was the rendered HTML of a live WordPress site, not a React project, and it was cut off at the paste limit. The site is a podcast's; it is not named in the repository, because the design is the friend's work for their client, and the owner holds the source. Following the porting guide's rule for code pasted without a licence, the owner's word that the friend gave it to them is the clearance, recorded here. None of the client's words, photographs, portraits or logo are used anywhere.

## Decision

1. **The live page is the source, read whole.** Its raw HTML, its rendered DOM at 1535, 1024, 768 and 390 pixels, its theme's stylesheet (every rule that matches the home page, found in the browser and kept with its media query, and the rules that selector matching missed read by hand), its theme's script (`main.min.js`, which drives the page with jQuery, GSAP 3.13 with ScrollTrigger, SplitText, DrawSVG and MotionPath, Lenis and an Owl carousel it does not use on the home page), its two Syne files and its pictures were saved and read. Every element's computed box and styles were dumped at the four widths, and the looping motion was sampled on the live page every tenth of a second for 46 seconds, because its GSAP tweens overlap in ways the code alone does not settle.
2. **A scoped stylesheet, not utilities.** The source is hand-written CSS in rems of a 10px root, so its rules are ported nearly one for one into `inegro.css`, scoped to `.inegro`, with `--u` as one of its rems (0.625rem) so each size reads as the source wrote it. Its WordPress blocks' flex gaps (0.5em and 2em), its columns' six per cent sides, their `min-width: 0` and their stacking below 782px are kept. The markup keeps the source's tree where it decides the layout (the card's photograph covers the whole band, not its inner column; the list's card is placed against its row). The two faces are written out from the preview's variables (`--template-font-display`, `--template-font-body`), because the theme's `--font-display` and `--font-body` are inlined into utilities and are not variables a stylesheet can read.
3. **Eleven blocks in the source's order, mapped to a business's words.** The header with two dropdowns; the hero; three glass cards (intro, approach, mission, the source's introduction, host and institute); the list of guests as the list of services (three to six); the quotes that travel the ribbons as three principles and the company's own statement over its name; the community ring's figures as the three steps, numbered 01 to 03 by the template so nothing is claimed; the resources as the three value propositions; the Substack form as a form that mails the owner (ADR 0008's rule for forms); the gradient band as the closing ask; the footer. The only optional piece is the band's row of social links. The header's first dropdown lists the services and its second the three glass cards; both show the first two services as picture cards, as the source's showed its two latest episodes. Nine image slots: the three glass cards' photographs and six services'.
4. **The token map.** The source's night violet is `surface`, its white `on-surface`, the grey of its closed names `on-surface-muted`, its purple `brand` and `brand-deeper` with white as `on-brand`, its blue and green the two glows. Text over a photograph sits on the `scrim` and is `on-scrim`, which a brand's set keeps dark and white in both schemes, so a light page still reads its photo bands. The source's four other hues (peach and yellow ribbons, lilac and sand boxes) and its two coloured buttons (blue with the ink on it, green with white) are a palette derived from the tokens (`--inegro-*`: the glows, two hues turned from them where the browser can, and pastel mixes), which the example sets to the source's own values through `--inegro-set-*`. The two coloured buttons default to the fill that carries `on-brand`, so a brand's buttons always pass. The nav links (the source's white at 40 per cent) are the muted ink, and the open service's line (its white at half opacity) a fifth of the way from the muted ink to the ink: the same greys on the source's page, and solved to AA on a brand's, where an alpha cannot be.
5. **Motion plays as the source's does, measured against it.**
   - The hero: the ribbons' box grows from half size over 2.5s while the strip under it pans over 4s; each ribbon draws itself over its own time after its own wait (DrawSVG from nothing, GSAP's default `power1.out`, which is exactly a cubic bezier since it is quadratic), as stroke dashes on a unit path length; a light runs along each twice on a sine (SMIL on the gradients' ends, since CSS cannot move a gradient's attributes), hidden under reduced motion; the bar and the words arrive at two seconds over 1.5s. Frame sheets of both pages a second apart from first paint match.
   - The glass cards' paragraphs light letter by letter between the card's top at the middle of the screen and the card's content height and half a screen later, chasing the scroll with GSAP's 0.75s expo lag, and stay lit (`motion-lit.ts`). The chase runs in the frame as GSAP's ticker ran it: each frame first carries the running chase on to its own time, then reads the scroll and restarts from there toward a new target, so the letters move on by a frame's worth every frame of a smooth scroll. Each letter is a span, lit or not, as the source's `gsap.set` coloured each letter it reached: a frame marks only the letters the chase has newly reached or left, so it restyles one or two, not the paragraph's hundreds. The letters are hidden from readers, who get the text whole.
   - The block after each glass card starts 165px up under it and slides out over the 550px of scroll after the card's foot reaches the screen's, at three tenths of the scroll (`motion-stack.ts`), with the screen's height taken at load as the source took it; the source left a block where it was if the page jumped (after a jump to the list it leaves the list tucked under the card, which then covers two of the names), this clamps.
   - The notes travel the two ribbons as the source's did, in turn and at an even pace: 2s in to a fifth of the way along, 10s at rest, 1.5s out to halfway, the next setting out, the last staying; a sheen crosses the purple ribbon once after the first arrives (`motion-notes.ts`). A card's centre is placed on the drawn path by its length, as MotionPath placed it, with the ribbons' place in the band measured once when the run begins, as MotionPath aligned once, and nothing written while a card rests. Sampled against the source every half second, the positions agree to the pixel along the path.
   - The ring drifts on the source's twelve-second loop (the outline circle 600 units out and back, the right dot 80, the left dot waiting, dipping and swinging, as sampled), from when the block's top reaches the middle of the screen, and its conic ring turns every five seconds on a sine from load, as the source's did; its three lines light at ten letters a second from a second into each turn, hold two seconds and fade over 1.5s, on their own clock from three seconds after load (`motion-ring.ts`).
   - The closed names' letters roll up to a white copy under the pointer above 1024px (the source's script split them only when the window was wider than 1024), a hundredth of a second apart for the first 26 (the source's stylesheet staggered no more; later letters rise at once), over 0.6s on the source's curve; the bar hides on the way down and returns as gradient glass on the way up; a dropdown, and on the phone a section of the sheet, slides open over 0.6s on jQuery's swing, and opening another hides the open one at once, as the source's one handler did for both; the veil and the phone's sheet open as the source's did. Swing and GSAP's `sine.inOut` are the same half cosine, drawn as `cubic-bezier(0.37, 0, 0.63, 1)`, within 0.2 per cent of it (the more common 0.445, 0.05, 0.55, 0.95 strays by 1.75, six degrees of the ring's turn). The newsletter field's border eases over 0.15s and its fill changes at once, as the source's form plugin set them.
   - Under reduced motion nothing moves: every letter is lit, the last note rests where it would have stopped (placed by the stylesheet from the ribbon's geometry, with no script), and the ring shows its first line.
6. **Deviations, all deliberate.** No cookie bar and no search (the bar's search button is the ask, outlined, with an arrow for its magnifier); no Lenis of its own (the site's smooth scroll stands, as for every port, but at the source's weight: the root carries `data-scroll-lerp="0.1"`, the source's Lenis lerp, which the site's smooth scroll reads on each page, where the site's own is 0.075; every scroll-tied motion here is timed against that glide); the newsletter field keeps the site's visible focus (a brand-coloured border and outline) where the source showed its form plugin's thin blue ring; the intro's play mark is an arrow in the same white disc, since nothing plays; the source's cut-out portraits are not ported (a service card has no guest, and the pipeline has no cut-outs), and with them the frosted panel behind each and the host card's portrait; the list's card shows the brand's image logo where the source showed its mark, and nothing for a wordmark; the brand's name is on the card's tab; the list opens and closes with buttons carrying `aria-expanded`; Escape and a press on the veil close a dropdown; the hover colour of the source's blue button, which set its ink on the ink, is the scrim's white; the bar's phone layout holds to 1024px inclusive, as the source's did; the footer's small print is the photographers' credit the Pexels licence asks for.
7. **Every screen, where the source broke on some.** The owner asked on 1 October 2026 for the template to be "fully responsive". Where the source's layout fails at a size, the port departs from it at that size only and is the source's to the pixel elsewhere:
   - Its phone rules end just short of 768px and its desktop rules begin just past 1024px, so a tablet held upright or on its side gets one layout whole. The source ended its phone rules at 768px and began tablet rules there too, and began some desktop rules at 1024px under a phone bar; at exactly those widths both applied, which broke the newsletter's heading inside a word at 768px and, in the port as first built with the source's boundaries, mixed the list's card, the logo and the ribbons at 1024px.
   - The hero is at least the screen's height rather than exactly it, and grows with its words; the source's 30px nudge of the centred words is padding on tablets and desktops, which centres them in the same place and counts toward that growth. On a phone on its side and on a short laptop window (1280 by 600, 1366 by 657) the source's headline ran up under the bar and its pills down past the hero.
   - The phone's sheet scrolls as a whole when it does not fit, rather than scrolling its links alone above pinned picture cards, which on a phone on its side left the links no room and put the ask below the screen; while it is open the header carries `data-lenis-prevent`, so a wheel over it scrolls the sheet and not the page behind, which the site's smooth scroll otherwise moved under the sheet despite the page's `overflow: hidden`.
   - Between 782 and 1024px the three offer boxes have the phone's 25px sides, not 45px, and hyphenate a word still too long; the source's 110px column broke its headings inside words.
   - A travelling note rests at the band's side on a phone too narrow for it to centre on its ribbon (below about 350px), in the script and in the stylesheet's resting place alike, which agree to the pixel. The script moves the card in by as much as its rest needs, growing that nudge over its arrival and shrinking it over its departure, so it glides there without a jump (no visible frame moves it more than 4px). Wherever it fits there is no nudge, and the card comes in from beyond the band's side and leaves past the other, as the source's did.
   - Any word too long for its line breaks rather than overflow (`overflow-wrap: break-word` on the root), for a brand's words longer than the example's.

## Consequences

- Nine templates are registered; Inegro is `ready: false`, so the selector still chooses three of the eight and a returning email still meets ADR 0038's implicit two-visit cap. Making it ready needs an explicit cap on visits first. `templates/render.tsx` renders it, so a concept could show it once it is ready.
- Verified on 30 September 2026 against the live page, section by section, with its stacking shifts switched off and its letters lit so both show their resting layout: at 1535px the header, hero, services, notes, process, offers, mission, form and band match its rendered heights to the pixel; the intro differs by one line of the example's copy, the approach card by the source's portrait (37px), the footer by the credit's length. At 390px the hero, services, notes, process and form match; the rest differ by the example's copy. At 1024px every block matches but the copy-bound ones; exactly 768px is a width where the source's own desktop and phone rules both apply and it breaks its headline mid-word, which the port no longer copies (decision 7). The frame sheets of the hero's first seven seconds, the scroll frames of the intro's letters and the list sliding out, the notes' positions over fifteen seconds and the ring's loop agree with the source.
- Named Prism while it was built, renamed Inegro by the owner on 30 September 2026.
- The owner, shown the first build: "we are missing many of the text animation within the site". Two things were checked. (a) Everything that moves on the source was recorded in a browser (every style, class and attribute a script changed and every CSS transition and animation that started, through the load, a slow scroll to the foot and back, and a hover over every link, button and heading) and the same on the port, and the two lists compared: the source has no text motion beyond the letters that light, the names' roll, the ring's lines and the hero's arrival, all of which the port had; its conic ring turned from load where the port waited for the block, now fixed. (b) Both pages were filmed scrolling at a person's pace with the site's smooth scroll on, and there the port's letters stood still while the page moved and lit only once it stopped: the first chase restarted at each scroll event's own time, and a smooth scroll sends one every frame, so no time ever passed. The step-wise scroll the first checks used had hidden it. With the chase in the frame (decision 5), the films of the whole page agree with the source's frame by frame. Vector's scrubbed entrances (`templates/t08-vector/sections/scrubs.tsx`) restart their chase the same way and are likely to stall the same way.
- The owner, shown the second build: some animations were "not fully optimised like the code I provided". Measured on 30 September and 1 October 2026 against the live page, the port as a production build, the same scripted input on both. (a) Cost: Chrome's counters over a load, a person-paced scroll through the intro, the notes and ring at rest, the rest of the page, and a hover over the names. The port had restyled every letter of a lit paragraph on every scroll frame (one variable on the paragraph, read by every letter) and read layout between writes in the stack and the notes. After the rewrite (decision 5) its script, layout and task time are at or under the source's in every phase: the intro's scroll takes 519 to 535ms of main-thread tasks to the source's 844 to 870, the rest of the page 671 to 741 to 1,303 to 1,310. A roll costs the same as the source's, 14 to 17ms of styling per name, measured one name at a time; the source's names showed less only in the profile that jumped to them, because the source then covers two of them and they never roll. (b) Exactness, each checked in a browser against the source: the glide now settles as the source's does (1,592 and 1,599 against 1,593 and 1,599 in the same wheel run), the letters light within a per cent of it at every sample, the ring's lines light, hold and fade on the source's timings (the only differences are the number of letters in each line: 0.1s each), and the dropdowns open on swing to two decimals and switch as the source's do. The source's text is `gsap.set` per letter, so lit or not, never between. The load's one slow frame on the port is its first layout, before first paint; after it the port drops fewer frames than the source.
- A second measurement on 1 October 2026 (three runs each at 4x CPU, Chrome's trace per phase) found one cost left: a paragraph whose letters were all lit was then marked done, which recoloured it and restyled about a thousand elements in one task, a frame over 34ms in two runs of three where the source had none. Every letter already holds its light by then, so the mark is gone; the intro's scroll now drops no frame over 34ms, and its style time is 166 to 170ms to the source's 209 to 219.
- Decision 7 was verified on 1 October 2026 on a production build: at 22 widths from 320 to 2560px nothing scrolls sideways, sits past the screen's edge or spills from its box, and text overlaps only where the ring's three lines share their place by design; at 580, 768, 782, 1024, 1220 and 1280px every element matches its layout a pixel either side; the hero's words clear the bar and stay inside it at 15 sizes, with identical positions wherever they fitted before; the sheet's links and ask are reachable by touch and by wheel at 320 by 568, 844 by 390, 1000 by 560 and the tablets, with the page still behind it; the notes stay on screen at 320px. The small links under 24px pass WCAG 2.5.8 by their spacing or as links in a sentence.
- The first build rendered every word in the site's own face, because the stylesheet read `--font-display` and `--font-body`, which the theme does not emit; section heights matched regardless, since every line height is fixed. A probe of the computed faces found it; the porting guide now says so.
- axe (WCAG 2.1 AA and best practice) finds only contrast, and only the source's own colours on the example: white on its purple (4.27:1), white on its green button (2.39:1), and, before the fix in decision 4, its nav at 40 per cent white (3.72:1). On a brand's set the declared pairs are solved to AA and the green button is the brand's fill. The dropdown's card text (the ink at half strength on white) and the glass cards' photographs are the source's; a photograph under a glass card is the one thing whose contrast a brand's set cannot guarantee.
- `docs/claims-register.md` row "A person designs every layout" still waits on the owner (ADR 0030): Inegro is one more layout designed by someone else and adapted here.
- The palette's two turned hues need relative colour syntax (Chrome 119, Safari 18, Firefox 128); without it they fall back to the glows.
- Owner decisions, each standing on the default above until the owner says otherwise: (1) the explicit visit cap, and then `ready: true`; (2) whether the friend is content with the credit as written in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`, which does not name the site; (3) whether the service card should carry a frosted panel of its own in place of the portrait's.

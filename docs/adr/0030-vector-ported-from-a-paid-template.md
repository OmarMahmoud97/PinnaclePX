# Vector, ported from a paid template's demo: React Bits Pro's Agency as t08

- Status: accepted
- Date: 16 September 2026
- Builds on: ADR 0008 (the template contract), ADR 0023 (the three ports and their rules), ADR 0027 to 0029 (the ports from published builds), `docs/template-porting-guide.md`

## Context

The owner pasted the rendered page of React Bits Pro's Agency template for `t08`, "not missing any details, animations, images, anything", and asked for it as the eighth layout. The port was made from the template's public demo at rbp-agency-template.vercel.app rather than from any purchased source, as a re-implementation of the design against this project's own tokens, contract and copy slots.

An earlier revision of this record treated the source's own licence as a constraint on the repository and held `t08` back from being committed while the repository is public. The owner directed on 18 September 2026 that the restriction does not apply to them and asked for it to be lifted, with the design left exactly as built. This record is amended accordingly: the port is committed with the other seven, and nothing about the template's markup, styles, shaders or motion changed in doing so. The two shaders were re-implemented from the demo's own GLSL when the owner, shown the first port, said some animations were "not exactly the same" and asked for every one to be checked; that check is decision 5 and the consequences below.

## Decision

1. **The demo is the source, read whole.** The HTML, the stylesheet (Tailwind 4; two themes of six variables each, `.dark` the default by the theme script; Geist loaded by next/font but never applied, because the theme block defines `--font-sans` as itself; a serif stack for the italic accents; a reduced-motion rule; a skip link and a focus ring) and the chunks (the providers: Lenis at 1.6s with anchors offset by 100px and the contact link scrolling to the foot, a theme provider, an overlay context; every block as a client component with its motion library props and GSAP timelines; the hero's wave shader with its three start and three end colours; the projects' ripple shader with its duotone and its circular mask; the cursor's springs; the marquee's velocity coupling) were saved, pretty-printed, split by module and read in full. Nothing was taken from a screenshot or from memory, and the demo was screenshotted with motion on so the stand-ins could be checked by eye.
2. **Every block is ported, class for class, with the tree the source has.** Nine blocks in the source's order: the bar and its menu pill, the hero, the projects (cursor, overlay, marquee, cards), the pinned sentence and the flowing menu, About, the bento of social proof, the FAQ and the sticky footer. The source is Tailwind 4, so every utility is kept verbatim (`max-w-360`, `2xl:max-w-450`, `3xl:max-w-550`, `text-[clamp(3rem,8vw,12rem)]`, `aspect-4/3`, `aspect-21/9`, `lg:grid-rows-[minmax(220px,auto)_minmax(220px,auto)_minmax(180px,auto)]`, `h-[1.5px]`); only its container and padding are written out as recipes.
3. **The token map for a monochrome page with two themes.** Its background is `surface`, its foreground `on-surface`, its muted (the cards' fill at half strength) `surface-muted`, its muted-foreground `on-surface-muted`, and every alpha of either the same alpha on the token. Its buttons, its lit menu rows, its cursor and its whole footer are the page inverted, painted `surface` on `on-surface`, so the template stays monochrome under any brand and the brand's colour lives in the waves. Its two floating pills are a dark glass in both themes (neutral-900 at 70 percent with white on it): `scrim` and `on-scrim`. The three colours of its wave shader (#FF66B2, #994DE6, #4D80FF) are `glow`, `glow-secondary` and `brand`; the duotone on its project pictures runs from a deep tint of the second glow on the surface to a pale tint of the glow. The example's two sets are the source's own two themes by hand, dark by default and `?scheme=light` for its light theme.
4. **Two pieces are optional, not absent.** The whole bento of social proof (a quote with a name, a role and a company, two figures, a rating and a story) and the footer's places and social links need facts the brief does not hold, so they are `| null`, never written by the copy stage, and filled on the example from the source's own copy. The footer's giant address is the owner's email when it is known, and its button leads there; the footer's list of services is the page's own. Five image slots: About's picture and up to four projects'; the bento's two pictures are example-only.
5. **Motion plays as the source's does, measured against it frame by frame.** On load the bar drops in and the headline's lines rise out of clipped rows in three dimensions on the source's curves, waits and lengths, the paragraph after them and the hint at two seconds. Below, the source scrubbed most entrances to the scroll with GSAP between a start and an end line of the viewport, with `scrub: 1`: the entrance does not follow the scroll but chases it, an expo tween of one second restarted at every scroll step. A small controller does the same (`sections/scrubs.tsx`): each `[data-scrub]` block names its lines, its trigger (itself, its section or its grid), its curve and its lag, and every frame the chased progress is eased and written as opacity, translate and scale; a group with a stagger follows one timeline as GSAP's did. The stylesheet's scroll-driven animations with the same lines stay as the fallback the controller switches off, and every view timeline's inset is set to nothing, because a view timeline otherwise takes the page's `scroll-padding-top` as its inset and every line sits a header's height early. GSAP's `power2` is the cubic curve and `power3` the quartic (it counts from quad), so those are the curves, sampled into `linear()` easings with the closest beziers as fallbacks. The pinned sentence is a sticky inner inside a block two and a half screens tall, its letters growing from their feet on the block's own timeline (`scrub: true`, no lag), each a twentieth of a second after the last over half a second on the quartic-in. A project picture is the source's shader, word for word, on a raw WebGL canvas (`sections/ripple.tsx`): the picture covering its frame, run through the source's duotone, shown through a circle whose radius follows the raw scroll from the card's top at four fifths of the viewport to a fifth above it (the source read the trigger's own progress for this, with no lag), feathered over 35px and roughened by drifting noise, and bent by ripples: each move of the pointer of more than four pixels drops a soft disc into a half-size buffer where thirty discs grow, turn, fade and add, and the buffer's red displaces the picture's sampling; the colour is encoded to sRGB as the source's renderer did. The source's bloom pass (a third of its lights above two thirds luminance, blurred over three mip levels) is the one piece not ported. The hero's waves are the source's shader, word for word, on a raw WebGL canvas (`sections/waves.tsx`): its three bands from the glow, the second glow and the brand, turning with the first screen of scroll, swept in over three and a half seconds, lit by passing beams, lined and vignetted, additive on a dark surface and multiplied on a light one, at the source's buffer scale. Where WebGL is missing, three drawn bands and a stylesheet mask stand in. The picture leans towards the pointer and swells under it on the source's two curves; its title and line play when the card's top passes the middle (the title over 1s on the quartic-out, the line over 0.8s on the cubic-out from 0.4s) and reverse as one timeline when it leaves upward: the line first, the title from 0.2s, each on its curve mirrored. The cursor follows the pointer on the source's spring (stiffness 400, damping 30, mass 0.2, stepped in slices of four milliseconds so a long frame cannot throw it, the pointer's place handed over on the next frame as the source's was), stretching with speed and turning with direction while its word stays upright; the marquee runs at 80px a second, to the right at rest, faster with the scroll's speed and the other way while the page scrolls up, on the source's smoothing spring; a menu row's lit face is set at once beyond the edge the pointer enters over and slides to rest over 0.6s on the source's expo, its letters hopping in turn (0.15s up on a sine, 0.2s down on a sine in and out, a hundredth apart), and slides out over the edge left by; the menu pill unfolds on a grid row with its links sliding in a twentieth apart and folds the same way in reverse; a project opens full screen over a dark wash with its title sliding in and a frosted close disc, and closes the same way in reverse; the bar goes while it is open and Escape closes it; the FAQ rows open on a grid row with a plus that turns; the footer sticks under the page from lg. Blocks are hidden only with motion allowed and, where a script must mark them, only with scripting enabled; nothing is tied to the scroll where the browser cannot tie it.

## Consequences

- Eight templates are ready; the selector chooses three of eight per submission. The registry test's "template without a contract" case moved from `t08-vector` to `t09-linen`.
- Verified on 16 September 2026 against the demo: every one of the eight blocks matches the demo's rendered height to the pixel at 1535px and at 390px, and the screenshot pairs match, the shaders included (the demo's pictures glow a little more at their brightest points, which is the bloom). The demo renders in the browser's own sans, not Geist, because its theme block defines its sans as itself; the example matches what the demo shows, since the owner compares by eye, and a brand's own body face takes the same variable.
- The motion was measured against the demo rather than judged by eye, with scratch Playwright scripts that sample the same element every frame on both pages across the same action and print the curves side by side. The headline's rise, the paragraph, the hint and the bar on load; a scrubbed entrance's chase after a jump of the scroll (the statement and the picture); a title and its line both ways; the cursor's arrival, stretch and settling and its going; a menu row lighting from below and going out over the top, and its letters' hop; the pill's unfold: each agrees with the demo within a frame or two at every sampled time. The lines every scrubbed entrance runs between, fitted from slow wheel passes down and then up on both pages, agree within two percent of the viewport. The pinned sentence agrees at six depths of the pin (23 letters grown, 8 growing and 25 flat at the middle, on both). The first port had three things wrong that this found: it used the cubic and quadratic curves for GSAP's `power3` and `power2`, which are the quartic and the cubic; its view timelines took the page's `scroll-padding-top` as their inset, so the letters ran a header's height early; and its title and line fell together on their rising curves instead of in reverse order on the mirrored ones. Its cursor spring also blew up on a long frame, and its scrubbed entrances followed the scroll instead of chasing it.
- Two findings about the demo itself. Its menu rows never slide in: their triggers were created in the rows' effects before the section's pin, so they were measured a screen and a half too high and the slide finished before the rows came into view; the port's rows stand still to match. And its scrolling is Lenis with a 1.6s expo glide on each wheel step, where this site's Lenis is a lerp (ADR 0021), so the demo's page feels heavier under the wheel than the example; the site's choice stands.
- With motion allowed, thirty-two checks pass: the bar drops and the lines rise over 1.6s from 0.3s, the hint fades in at two seconds, the waves are drawn by the shader on a canvas; the pill unfolds with six links, the plus turns, Escape folds it, and the pill names the block the page is at; the marquee slides on its own; the cursor appears over a project, is round at rest and stretches while it moves, and goes when the pointer leaves; a project's picture is drawn by its shader once seen; a picture leans and swells to 1.22; a title rises past the middle and falls back, the line falling first; a project opens full screen with the bar gone and the page still, and Escape closes it; the sentence stays pinned at the top while its letters grow, all grown by the end; a menu row lights from below, its letters hop, and it goes out over the edge left by; About's picture settles from nine tenths tied to the scroll by the script, and a jump past an entrance's end is chased over a second; the bento's cards arrive one after another; the questions open on a grid row, more than one at once, and close again; the footer sticks under the page; without WebGL three bands stand in and the picture opens through the stylesheet's circle, shut before the card reaches four fifths, 360px at the middle, open a fifth above; under reduced motion every part is visible and still, and with JavaScript off nothing waits on a script; at 1920 and 390 in both schemes there is no horizontal overflow and no console error. Measuring needed two things worth knowing: headless Chromium draws WebGL on software GL at three frames a second, so every timing is wrong unless it is launched with the GPU flags; and the site's smooth `scroll-behavior` turns a programmatic jump into a glide, so the scripts inject `scroll-behavior: auto` first.
- axe (WCAG 2.2 AA tags) is clean at 1920 and 390 in both schemes. The source's footer small print is its background at 40 percent on the inverted footer (2.69:1 on the light footer of the dark theme); the port has it at 60 percent, the one colour changed from the source.
- Deviations from the source, all deliberate: the theme switch is not ported (a business page has one scheme, as ADR 0023 decided for a toggle); the source's Lenis is not ported (its 100px anchor offset is `scroll-mt-25` on every anchored block); the FAQ carries an id (`faq`) and the menu's fifth link leads to it, where the source's led to the bento; the logo links to the top of the page; the projects' cursor is hidden from readers and each card is a labelled button that also opens on Enter and Space; the overlay is a labelled dialog whose close disc takes focus; each question's button carries `aria-expanded` and each closed answer is inert; the footer's "Created with passion by Pulsewave" is a slot, its legal line is "© year, legal name" with the photographers' credit after it, and its big address is the owner's, with the button leading to the top when none is known; the services block loses the source's `overflow-hidden`, which the browser counts as a scroll container and which would stop the sticky pin and the letters' timeline; the source's `skip-to-content` link is the site's own; the footer's small print is at 60 percent rather than 40; the pictures' bloom pass is not ported; the demo's own Lenis is not ported, the site's driving the page instead.
- `docs/claims-register.md` row "A person designs every layout" still needs the owner's decision: seven of the eight ready layouts were designed by other people and adapted here.
- The porting guide gains: confirm the source's rendered faces and not only the port's; a GSAP scrub with `scrub: true` becomes a scroll-driven animation with `cover` lengths, one with a number becomes a chasing controller, pins become sticky blocks with `contain` ranges, growing masks become registered lengths, every view timeline gets a zero inset, no ancestor of any of them may clip, toggling entrances become observer-marked transitions that reverse in the timeline's order on mirrored curves, GSAP's powers count from quad, a trigger created before its parent's pin may never be seen to run, and motion is measured on both pages frame by frame with the GPU flags and without smooth scrolling.

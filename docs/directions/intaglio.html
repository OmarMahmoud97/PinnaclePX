What changes on a visitor's page

  • No figures, and no invented facts. The run becomes an 'At a glance' sheet of at most three rows, each restating a phrase of the owner's sentence. A row with no source is dropped, never padded.
  • The body's bottom line is the business's signature on the double rule, never an invented total. The ask stays on the stub, beside the seal.
  • A trust line of at most two items, never filler and never repeating a row. One item, or none, is fine.
  • One photo, if there is one, sits inside the body's top corner at 3:2, lazy and small, so the headline stays the LCP. With no photo the title takes the row.
  • Faces and scheme come from the visitor's style. The figure face loads only for a digit the owner typed.

What the stress cases show

  • A long name wraps with balanced lines at 15 px in the phone bar and never breaks inside a word; the bar keeps the ask and the Menu toggle at 44 px.
  • An owner-typed digit. '45 minutes' came from the sentence, so rules.ts lets it through and the figure face prints the 45. DM Sans cannot set tabular figures; the third variable can.
  • One trust item, two rows. The sentence had six facts; the rest are already on the page, so nothing is padded.
  • The fallback takes the first sentence's first clause as the headline when the sentence is longer than 90 characters, seals the rest of it as a note, and keeps a trust signal in the first screen with a fixed claim-free line. It repeats no words. Only a sentence with no clause of 12 to 90 characters falls back to the name.

Plate 7

The signature: the seal on the tear line, and the print run

One WebGL moment, at the hero's own scale. The seal's rings are drawn analytically in a shader from the same formula as the static SVG, r = R + A cos(kθ − 2πi/N), in brand-deeper, on-surface-muted and brand, read from the tokens. It starts on the first sign of intent, sweeps one highlight round the rim and stops pointing at the ask, and then the ask's arrow nudges once: the last thing that moves is the button. Below 64rem, where the seal and the ask are never on screen together, it stays SVG.

The static fallback: the first paint for everyone, and the whole design under reduced motion, without WebGL, with the flag off, after context loss, in print and in forced colours.

  1. 0 msFirst paint: headline, lead, ask and trust line, plus the SVG seal on the tear. Nothing is hidden and nothing waits for a script.
  2. First intentA pointer move, scroll or key, from 64rem only, arms it. The canvas cross-fades in over the identical SVG in 240 ms.
  3. 0 to 1.4 sThe monogram's relief rises and one highlight sweeps round the rim on the spring, one and three-quarter turns, and stops pointing left, at the ask.
  4. 1.4 to 2.1 sThe ask's arrow nudges once, 6 px and back. Then nothing draws.
  5. On the askHover, focus or press deepens the relief from 1 to 1.25 in 140 ms, released on leave. Pointer movement rakes the light across the relief.
  6. Hero goneThe canvas fades back to the SVG; program, buffer and texture are deleted and the context is lost on purpose. It is never re-created.

The print run

Rules draw, figures land, the total lands last, then the ask. Each leader draws from label to value in 0.5 s and its value settles 6 px, 70 ms apart; the two strokes of the double rule draw 60 ms apart; the total rises inside a clipped row; the stub settles 8 px; the ask's arrow nudges once. About 2.3 s, played once when the schedule enters the viewport, never scrubbed. CSS keyframes only, transform and opacity, on one critically damped spring sampled into linear(). Under reduced motion every rule is drawn and every figure shown.

Plate 9

Nine sections in the order the buyer asks

What is it, does it work, what does it cost, is our money safe, how hard is switching, who else uses it. Every ask moves down to one form, and none points back up.

SectionIts job, and where it asksOn a visitor's page

  1. header

    Wayfinding and the ask at every width, the 375 bar included. Outlined while any in-page ask is in view.

    Logo or display-face wordmark; askShort of 14 characters or fewer.

  2. intro

    The counterfoil: value, first ask and one trust line on the stub; the payment run on the body; the seal on the tear beside the ask.

    'At a glance' in at most three sourced rows; the body ends on the signature.

  3. terms

    Fees second, because hidden charges are the category's main fear. The whole arithmetic, then the ask on the double rule.

    Only the terms left over after the hero, in words; otherwise null.

  4. offer

    Three drawn fragments prove the product is specific: a checked payee, two signatures, an invoice matched to a payment.

    The brief's offers; a 3:2 detail photo, or a specimen plate when there is none.

  5. assurance

    Where the money sits and who can move it: a register of plain rows, documents, and 'Request the security pack'.

    'What you can hold us to' only when the sentence states commitments; otherwise null.

  6. process

    Switching feels small: one rule, three ticks, and the rule ends at the ask.

    The brief's three steps; timings null.

  7. proof

    Results for firms like theirs, as corrections: was a day now 20 minutes. Two signed statements and a ledger follow.

    Worded corrections only when the sentence states a before and after; otherwise null.

  8. contact

    The last doubts beside the booking form, which stays in view when it fits the screen. The only conversion.

    Name, email and message; the demo state, and the seal stamps.

  9. footer

    Wayfinding, contact, and the regulatory slot at 16 px, marked as a placeholder.

    The slot is null and never generated.

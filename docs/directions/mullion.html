Why the first screen converts

What and where (the kicker deck), the value (h1), the supporting line, a trust line and the ask, all server text. The coloured column is the only saturated thing on screen, so the eye lands where the visitor acts. The service index gives the foot of the h1 pane a job: three or four links down to #services, no claims.

On a visitor's page the trust line restates the brief, never a figure; only the example may say Rated 4.9 from 212 reviews. With no photo the hero's row B shows the company name as a wordmark (Pellow & Garth, below), never a repeat of the text beside it.

Small phones. At 375 × 548, Safari's visible height with its bars (an assumption to confirm on a device), the call pane's ask can fall below the fold on long briefs; there the header ask cell carries the first-screen ask. The measures are in the spec.

The close: nothing waits

The contact heading and the form are never hidden. The heading sits in the ask column on colour from the start; the form is a surface pane, legible on any ground. Only the decorative colour layer widens, from that column to the frame, behind content that is already there.

The widening is skipped when #contact is reached from any in-page link, when the address carries #contact, and when the block is already on screen as motion arms. Every row that intersects the viewport when motion arms is marked shown without animating, so the hero never flashes. Below 48rem there is no widening at all.

Palette

Example hex #0f7a6c, viridian; light is the default. Four grounds: clear (surface), tint (accent), muted (surface-muted) and colour (brand-deeper), which holds only asks. Ratios are the palette tool's, floored, each beside the worst over its 17-hex corpus.

Estimated share of a 1440 light page.

Surface 55%, brand-deeper 15% (header cell, call pane, the pane after the steps, the contact block), accent 10%, surface-muted 10%, rules and type the rest. Unused: brand, glow, glow-secondary, scrim, on-scrim. No gradients, shadows, radii or text on photos.

Buttons, links and chips

A Mullion button is a small window: a label light and a square arrow light split by a 1 px mullion in the text colour. Where it can, it is its pane's own bottom-right cell, flush to the pane's edges. Hover deepens the fill to brand-deepest in 150 ms, an opacity cross-fade on a pseudo-element; the inverse switches at once to on-brand on brand-deepest. Press scales to 0.98; focus is a 2 px ring inset 6 px in the button's text colour. Nothing is revealed by hover alone.

Live

Tab, hover and press these. The topic chips are a native radio group, so they work without JavaScript and submit a value. Each "Ask about this" carries its service in its accessible name, after the visible words.

The moment: tiles lean toward the ask

One WebGL canvas, in the hero's call pane, from 48rem only. Its rest frame is the plain pane, pixel for pixel, so at rest it is simply hidden and the text keeps its subpixel smoothing. On first intent a diagonal front of square tiles crosses the pane from its top-left, the headline's side, to the ask in the bottom-right corner in 1.4 s; as it lands, an L of tiles one module out from the ask lifts for 0.38 s, then all of it settles back to the plain pane and stops.

After that, tiles under a pointer lift a step and spring back, and hovering or focusing the ask lights the same L round it. Under text the tiles deepen toward brand-deepest; round the ask they lift toward on-brand, and the ring touching the ask never moves.

Real frames from that canvas

Drawn by the live shader at load, one frame each, copied out of the same context; the white block marks the ask and the bars mark the text lines, which are DOM over the canvas. The static state and the settled frame are the plain pane, so they match by construction.

What WebGL adds over CSS. A CSS mask sweeping a static tile pattern could draw the front at no script cost. WebGL adds the per-tile pointer response and the landing L, both quantised to whole tiles on one spring, and it reads the tokens live. If the owner reads it as decoration, the CSS sweep is the fallback plan.

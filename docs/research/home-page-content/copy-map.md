## 2. The voice fingerprint

Measured over the home page's visible copy as shipped (135 sentences, 994 words), transcribed from the files above into `copy-stats.mjs` beside this file and run on 5 September 2026. Observed by script; the Flesch-Kincaid figure uses a rough syllable heuristic and is approximate.

### Sentence length

| Measure                            | Value                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sentences                          | 135                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Mean length                        | 7.4 words                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Median                             | 6 words                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Longest                            | 20 words (the hero subhead's first sentence, exactly at the limit: "Answer a few short questions and about five minutes later, you'll see three homepage designs in your logo and colours.") |
| 1 to 5 words                       | 52 (39%)                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| 6 to 10 words                      | 51 (38%)                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| 11 to 15 words                     | 25 (19%)                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| 16 to 20 words                     | 7 (5%)                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Over 20                            | 0                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Flesch-Kincaid grade (approximate) | about 2.4 by the heuristic; treat as "grade 3 to 5". Well under the 7th-grade target.                                                                                                        |

What this means for new copy: the page is not "under 20 words"; it is **mostly under ten**. A new section written at 15 to 18 words a sentence will pass the test and still sound like a different writer. Aim for a median of 6 to 8 and keep one sentence in five at 12 to 17 words for rhythm.

### The fragment habit

26 of 135 sentences are one to three words and most have no finite verb. They do specific jobs:

- **The negated fear**: "Free." "No sign-up." "No phone number." "No budget question." "No pitch." "Not a salesperson." "Nobody chases you." Fifteen sentences begin No, Not, Nobody or Nothing. The page's signature move is naming the feared thing and removing it.
- **The one-word verdict that opens an answer**: "No." "Yes." "Partly, and we'll say where." "Not as it is." "There isn't one." Every answer in Straight answers and the FAQ opens with the verdict, then explains.
- **The unit**: "20 minutes." "On every design." "Five short questions."
- **The gentle invitation**: "Rather talk first?" "Not started yet?"

### Pronouns

- you / your / yours: 99. we / our / us: 29. Ratio about 3.4 to 1. Thirteen sentences start "You" or "Your"; ten start "We".
- First person singular (I, my, me) appears 9 times and **only in the visitor's mouth**: the button "Show me my three designs" and the questions ("What do you do with my email?", "What if I don't like any of them?", "Do I need a logo...?", "What will you ask me?", "Can I use one of the designs as my website?", "I already have a website."). The studio is always "we"; it is never "I" and never a name.
- When the human matters, the studio says "a person" or "the person" (three times). This is how the one-person fact is stated within the no-name rule.

### Rhythm and punctuation

- Contractions: 20 (we'll, we're, we've, you'll, don't, isn't, there's, what's, it's). Used in leads, bodies and answers; never in an H2, an H3 or a button.
- Questions: 14, all in the visitor's words and all answered directly under them.

- Zero exclamation marks. Zero em dashes (test-enforced). Zero semicolons. Three colons, each introducing a list.
- Numbers: counts in words (five, three, nine, one: 43 instances); measured units in digits (20-minute, 20 minutes, 30 days, Question 5 of 5: 7 instances). "Five minutes" is always in words. Rule: **counts in words, measurements in digits**, and anything from `CONFIG` renders as a digit.
- British English throughout: colours, ring, sort it out, chases you, a couple of minutes, typeface. No "organisation" yet on the page; the spelling rule stands.
- Sentence case everywhere: headings, buttons ("Book a 20-minute call"), nav, footer headings.
- Tense: present simple for what happens ("We send you your link."); present perfect for the pipeline already at work ("we've already started"); the future ("you'll see") appears once, in the hero.

### H2 rhythm: a correction to the brief

Not every H2 ends with a full stop. The pattern is two registers:

| H2                                                               | Register          | End       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- | --------- |
| "See your new website before you hire." (H1)                     | sentence          | full stop |
| "One question at a time."                                        | sentence          | full stop |
| "Five answers get you three designs. Imagine what an hour does." | two sentences     | full stop |
| "Straight answers."                                              | sentence-as-label | full stop |
| "About the studio"                                               | label             | none      |
| "Frequently asked questions"                                     | label             | none      |
| "Your three designs are five questions away."                    | sentence          | full stop |
| "What you get" (visually hidden)                                 | label             | none      |

Persuasive sections take a **sentence H2 with a full stop**; the two reference sections (About, FAQ) take a **noun-phrase label with no stop**. A new persuasive section (examples, the real build, why not a builder) should take a full sentence ending in a full stop; a reference block (after launch, a care plan table) may take a label. Card and step titles (H3) never take a full stop: "Three designs, not one", "You look at your three designs", "We build the site". Step titles are "You ..." or "We ..." clauses that read as a sequence.

### How a section opens

Every visible section is H2 plus one lead paragraph in `text-lead text-on-surface-muted`, in the left column of the 2/4 or 3/3 grid (`section-styles.ts:9`, `titleHeading`; the lead class is inline in each section file). Lead lengths: Straight answers 1 sentence; FAQ 1; How it works 2; Taster 5; About two paragraphs of 3. The lead says what the section is **in the visitor's words** and ends on the visitor's action or the studio's commitment ("Then we take the time to design and build the real thing, properly." "Anything else, ask on the call."). Below the lead: a list (cells, beats, steps, disclosures), then one action (a `TrackedLink` button) and one small-text line under it (the click trigger or a "you can also do nothing" line).

### The mono caption register and where it is used

`captionStyles` = `font-mono text-label text-on-surface-muted` (`components/ui/caption.ts:3`, comment: "The instrument register: captions, labels and measurements in Geist Mono. Never headings or body copy"). Observed uses:

Rule for new sections: the "Example brief, not a client" label, any "Design 01 to 03" thumbnail labels, a "true today" status, timings and counts go in mono caption. Nothing persuasive goes in mono.

### Honesty-label wording

The five captions follow one shape: **[what it is]. [what it is not].** "Sketch of an example brief. A first look, not one of the designs." "The same example brief, built as an illustration. Not a client, and not one of the designs." The denial names the two things a sceptic might mistake it for: a client, and a design. Planned labels from the prior docs, not yet in code: card eyebrow "Example brief, not a client" (plan 3.5, design plan 3.5, research 3); preview-page line "Made from a five-answer brief we wrote ourselves. Design plan 3.5 also fixes the verb: "This is what they look like" for hand-set example content; "This is what came back" only when the pipeline produced it.

### Error and status copy

Imperative plus reassurance, no "invalid", no "error": "Tell us a little more, a sentence or two is plenty." "That does not look like an email address." "Use a hex code such as #2F6F4E." "Your logo has not finished uploading. Give it a moment, or remove it." (`lib/brief/schema.ts:15-64`). Status is a participle in mono: "building", "on its way".

## 8. A short style sheet for whoever writes the new sections

Business reasoning distilled from sections 1 and 2; nothing here is new policy.

- Median sentence 6 to 8 words; nothing over 20; one longer sentence in five for rhythm.
- Open every answer with the verdict. Open every section with H2 plus one lead in the visitor's words.
- Name the fear, then remove it: "No X." is the page's cadence. Fifteen such sentences already exist; add sparingly or it becomes a tic.
- "You" three times for every "we". "I" only in the visitor's mouth. The studio is "we" or "a person", never a name.
- Counts in words, measurements in digits, `CONFIG` values rendered not typed.
- Persuasive H2s are sentences with a full stop; reference H2s are labels without one; H3s never end in a stop.
- Mono caption for labels, counts, timings and honesty labels; never for persuasion.
- Honesty label shape: what it is, full stop, what it is not.
- Terminology: the five questions, three designs, the live sketch, the taster, preview, brief, wording. Never copy, generate, instantly, AI (beyond the one cell), AI website builder.
- Every number and promise in a new section must be traceable to a row in section 1 or a decision in section 5. If it is not, it is not ready to ship.

# Competitor sweep: how builders and studios show examples, promise outcomes and sell the paid step

Prepared 5 September 2026 for the PinnaclePX home page plan. Three groups: the DIY builders the audience weighs, UK studios that sell websites to small businesses, and anyone who lets a buyer see a design before paying. Each row is labelled **observed (5 Sept 2026)** when the page was loaded today, or **Unverified** when the fetch failed and the row rests on a search snippet or nothing.

## 0. Method and its limits

- Pages were loaded with a text fetch that converts the page to markdown. Copy, captions, button labels, prices, FAQ questions and section order are reliable. Hover effects, scroll behaviour, video and device toggles are not visible in text; where a row says "carousel" or "device frames" it is inferred from Previous/Next controls, image names (for example "plants-desktop") or captions, and is marked "inferred".
- Search results are labelled "search snippet" and treated as **Unverified** until the page itself loaded.
- Every claim about a competitor below is what the page says about itself. None of it is evidence that the claim is true.
- Failed fetches are listed in section 6. Nothing was invented to fill a gap.
- "Shape" in the brief is MadeByShape's trading name (their own testimonial reads "Shape created something better than I ever could have imagined"), so the two names share one row. "Toast Design" is toastdesign.co.uk in Banbury; toast.co.uk redirects to a clothing retailer. Thomas Digital is in San Francisco, not the UK, so it sits in group 3.

### 1a. What the builders say about ownership, hosting and support (observed 5 Sept 2026)

- Hosting is always bundled and always "99.99% uptime" (Wix, 10Web, Framer, Relume). Support is "24/7" (Wix, Squarespace, Hostinger, 10Web). The domain is "free for one year" on an annual plan (Wix, Squarespace, Hostinger), which is a discount, not ownership.
- Only two builders say anything about owning the output: GoDaddy Airo ("your code, your choice") and 10Web ("a domain that's yours from day one"). Wix, Squarespace, Durable, Mixo and Hostinger say nothing about what happens when you stop paying. The UK studios in section 2d make that silence their main argument.
- Every builder except Relume and Framer leads with a time claim: 30 seconds (Durable), seconds (Mixo, 10Web, Hostinger), minutes (Wix, Mixo, Hostinger, GoDaddy). Relume's "Brief to live site in one flow" is the only one that names the input.
- Social proof is by volume: 3 million, 11M+, 750k+, 3M+, 2M+, 10M+, 5M+, 1m+. Not one builder shows the input that produced an example.

### 2a. How UK studios present examples (observed 5 Sept 2026)

- Evidence: the CAP Code, section 3 (https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html, **observed 5 Sept 2026**). Rule 3.33: comparisons "must compare products meeting the same needs or intended for the same purpose." Rule 3.34: they "must objectively compare one or more material, relevant, verifiable and representative feature of those products, which may include price." Rule 3.37: a comparison "with an unidentifiable competitor must not mislead... The elements of the comparison must not be selected to give the marketer an unrepresentative advantage." Rule 3.41: a comparison with an identifiable competitor "must not discredit or denigrate another product, marketer, trade mark, trade name or other distinguishing mark." Rule 3.7: marketers "must hold documentary evidence to prove claims that consumers are likely to regard as objective". Business reasoning: "looks like everyone else's" and "renting not owning" are opinions dressed as facts; "a person lays out your page", "you own the files", "there is no monthly fee to keep the site up" are verifiable features PinnaclePX can compare on.

## 4. Synthesis

### 4.1 Five presentation ideas that fit PinnaclePX's honesty rules and hairline system

1. **Label the invention and make the label the feature.** Sightmade is the only competitor that shows invented concepts and says so; Thomas Digital's rejects gallery proves an offer by exhibiting what did not sell. PinnaclePX's version: the examples band from docs/home-page-design-plan.md 3.5, with the mono caption "Example brief, not a client" on every card and a footnote "Every business here is invented. Nothing on this page is client work." With one template today: one card, VetPres or Go Wild, three viewports impossible, so show the one Aurora design at phone and desktop and say "One of the ten layouts. The other nine are being built." At three templates: one brief, three designs (the card the plan already specifies). At ten: three briefs by segment (a product, a studio, a trade) so the visitor picks the one nearest their business, which does the job Wix's "Good for:" line and Durable's trade carousel do without a filter. (Business reasoning; observed precedents above.)
2. **Show the input beside the output.** No competitor shows the brief that produced an example; Thomas Digital's "Before / Free Mockup / Final" strip is the nearest. PinnaclePX can put "The sentence we gave it:" in quotation marks, the style and palette chips from components/sketch, then the first viewport labelled Design 01 to 03. It is the hero loop's promise (sketch becomes page) kept with a real render, and it is the one thing the DIY builders cannot copy honestly because their input is a template, not a sentence. The caption must read "This is what they look like" until the pipeline itself produced the nine, then "This is what came back" (the plan's own rule). (Business reasoning.)
3. **"Open this design" opens the real thing.** Podium, dotwall, MadeReal, Mike and Oddology all send a hosted link "you can open on any device"; Relume pairs a live preview with the card. The examples band should link to the same route family the visitor will receive (/examples/aurora today; the preview route later) so the taster is demonstrably the product's own mechanism, not a picture of it. Mono caption under the frame: "Rendered from the template with the example brief." No "live", no "generated". The link is also the honest answer to the new objection "Can I see an example first?" (Observed precedents; business reasoning.)
4. **Phone first, desktop beside it, inside the frames the page already owns.** Wix, Mixo and Fit Design show desktop and mobile of the same design together; the audience is on a phone and the FAQ worry "Will my website work on smart phones and tablets?" appears verbatim at MadeByShape. PinnaclePX already has a browser frame and a phone frame in components/sketch; render the example thumbnails inside those frames with the corner ticks, phone frame first below md, so the examples read as the sketch grown up rather than as a new visual world. Thumbnails are static next/image renders captured by the plan's scripts/render-examples.ts, so nothing on the page is an iframe or a fake browser. (Observed precedents; the owner's refinement-not-redesign rule.)
5. **Name the scar, then state the scope, without a price.** The strongest single sentences in the UK set are Podium's "you end up paying a deposit to find out — and if you don't like the direction, you're already committed", Three Girls Media's "a proper, itemised quote before you commit to anything, not a 'from' price designed to get you on a call and then double", and KOTA's "Launch isn't the finish line". Every small-business studio that states scope does it as a short list (pages, mobile, forms, hosting, domain, who writes, how long, who owns it) and a timeline in weeks; Appero even says who pays for hosting and the domain and roughly what. PinnaclePX can run a "The real build" strip in the 2/4 grid with that list, a timeline range once the owner sets one, "you own the site, the design and the files" if the owner confirms, and an after-launch line ("hosting, updates and small edits, a text change, a new photo, new opening hours") in Mike Web Developer's concrete register, and still show no price, because the fixed quote on the call is already the page's answer. A features-only comparison (a person lays out your page; you own the files; nothing to keep paying to stay up) meets CAP 3.34 and avoids 3.41 without naming Wix. (Observed; evidence: CAP Code section 3.)

### 4.2 Five things every competitor does that PinnaclePX must not copy

1. **Speed claims and the builder vocabulary.** "Get online in 30 seconds" (Durable), "in seconds" (Mixo, 10Web, Hostinger), "instantly" (Mixo), "Live in minutes" (GoDaddy), "11M+ Websites generated" (Durable), "AI website builder" (Wix, Squarespace, 10Web). Each is a banned word for PinnaclePX and each one files the studio under "AI website builder" in the reader's mind. The page's own "about five minutes" and Relume-style "the AI does X, a person does Y" are the ceiling. (Observed; owner rules.)
2. **Volume as proof.** "Trusted by 3 million business owners", "10M+ websites created", "Trusted by 100+ businesses worldwide", "5.0 · 133 reviews", "51 five-star reviews", Trustpilot and Google widgets, logo walls (KOTA, Toast, Pixelbricks, ID Studio, The Web Kitchen, Fit Design), and stock photographs of a coach or a handyman (Durable). PinnaclePX has none of these and must not invent them; the honest substitute is the labelled example and the mechanism running in front of the visitor. Durable's counter reading "0+ Businesses built" shows what happens when the widget outlives the truth. (Observed; owner rules.)
3. **Outcome numbers without a source.** "25+ enquiries per month and adding over £250k+ in revenue" (Bemunchie), "400% increase in AI Overviews" (ID Studio), "83.14% increase in sales" (KOTA), "38% Revenue Increase" (Fit Design), "0.67% versus 3%" conversion (Click Success), "Most web agencies charge £2,000+" (Quick2Web). CAP 3.7 requires documentary evidence for objective claims; PinnaclePX's page says what a good site does (enquiries, bookings, found on Google, credible on a phone) in words, and Three Girls Media's "brings in enquiries while you're asleep" shows that a plain sentence carries the point. (Observed; evidence: CAP 3.7.)
4. **Price theatre.** "FLASH SALE ON ALL WEBSITE PACKAGES", "£2.99/mo" struck through from "$10.99", "75% off", "from only £299" five times, "Affordable Web Design from £995" above "start from only £825", "We will beat any like for like web design quote", "Free logo design included", credit meters. Price is off PinnaclePX's page by rule; the wider lesson is no urgency, no bait, no free extras, and no "from" figure that a call then doubles. (Observed; owner rules.)
5. **The controlled reveal and the soft lock-in.** SiteSwan's "Don't email it prior... All I need is a credit card number"; Thomas Digital's budget dropdown, qualifying call and "no legal right to the mockup"; a required phone number on every UK preview form but one; "free" designs that exist only while you pay £19 to £39 a month (Pagio, Quick2Web, FreeWebsiteUK, Mike); SoftwareAura's "you agree to receive communication". PinnaclePX's link is shareable for 30 days, asks for no phone number and no budget, and nobody calls unless the visitor books; the examples and the "real build" strip must keep saying so, and the not-ready visitor needs an exit that is not a form. Also: naming Wix or Squarespace to run them down ("monthly software tax", "looks like every other site") is CAP 3.41 territory and would read as fear. (Observed; evidence: CAP 3.41; owner rules.)

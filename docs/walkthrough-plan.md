# The walkthrough: five questions, one phone, scroll-driven, ending on a finished site

Prepared 6 September 2026. The owner's brief: replace the How it works animation. It should be as animated and as smooth as the hero, move through every stage from question 1 to question 5 rather than only from 4 to 5, use a made-up brand instead of VetPres, and by stage 5 look like a real, good website. Everything below was checked against the code, the motion ADRs (0005, 0006, 0021) and screenshots of the section at 1440 and 390 taken today. Nothing is built yet; this is the plan for approval.

## Built on 6 September 2026

Approved and built the same day (ADR 0025). Where the build departs from the text below, this is why:

- **The finished page arrives with the button, not with beat 3.** Beat 3 paints the look and the colour; the button carries stage 6, so the site assembles as the ask comes into view. The beats take a quarter of a screen per stage at `md` and up and less on a phone (`how-it-works.tsx`), rather than the third the plan gave them, which left too much empty column.
- **The reading line is clamped to three quarters of the viewport.** On a wide screen the sticky frame sits beside the beats and is not yet stuck when the first beat arrives, so a line tied to the frame's bottom alone fired stages 1 and 2 together the moment the section entered. `CONFIG.walkthrough.anchorShare` holds it.
- **The timeline module is not lazy.** It was tried as a dynamic import beside GSAP, and Turbopack folded it back into the page's shared chunk, so the split bought nothing and the import is static. The finished page, its font and its photographs are the lazy part, as planned.
- **The wordmark keeps the sketch's type at stage 3.** The serif would have put its font file in the server-rendered frame for every visitor; the type changes with the build, as the hero's does.
- **The caret is solid, not blinking.** A blinking tween would have had to end exactly where the hide begins; an editor shows a solid caret while typing anyway.
- **The dots and the glow now show.** Both are `-z-1` layers of a block with its own background, which painted over them; the block is `isolate` now. The hero's stage never had the problem because its section is isolated.
- **The chips prop.** The track hides the chips with `chipsClassName="hidden"`, the prop on main; a `chips={false}` from another session's uncommitted change was used first and a clean checkout failed to type check, which the worktree build in section 9 caught.
- **Captions** fit two lines at every size: "An example brief. A sketch, not one of the designs." and "Built as an illustration. Not a client, not one of the designs."; the kicker became "Garden design studio" to hold one line of the phone.

Checks: typecheck, lint, knip and Prettier clean; the unit suite passes apart from one failure in another session's uncommitted FAQ wording; the Playwright suite passes apart from the four accessibility scans on desktop and tablet, which wait for a still page and are held open by that session's logo marquee (the only running animation on the page, probed), not by anything here; the contact sheet at 1440 and 390 is in the pull request. Bytes are in ADR 0025.

## 1. What is wrong today, precisely

- **The stage never walks.** `how-it-works-track.tsx` sets the stage to "the last beat on screen". At 1440 by 900 the heading, all three beats and the button fit in one screen (screenshot, today), so the moment the section arrives every beat is on screen and the stage is 5. The only thing that ever animates is the 900 ms timer between stage 4 and stage 5. On a phone the beats do scroll past one at a time, but the sticky strip is masked to 160 px, so the visitor sees only the top of the frame.
- **The transitions are not authored.** Each stage is a React re-render of `PhoneSketch`: a keyed remount plays the 300 ms `sketch-in` fade and the colour variables transition. Nothing travels, nothing crosses, nothing is timed against anything else. The hero, by contrast, is a GSAP timeline with FLIP travel, crossfades and staggers (`hero-loop.ts`). The two frames on one page look like two different products.
- **Stage 3 is skipped** (`DEMO_STAGES` is 1, 2, 4, 5: a wordmark has nothing to paint), so even a visitor who scrolls slowly sees four states, not five.
- **It ends on a wireframe.** The payoff beat says "Your three designs appear on screen" beside a grey sketch with a photo in it.

## 2. The idea in one paragraph

The phone stays sticky beside the copy, and the visitor's own scrolling advances one question at a time, exactly as the heading promises. Every advance is an authored GSAP transition (the hero's vocabulary: `power3.inOut` travel, crossfades that meet in the middle, staggers of 40 to 70 ms), never a re-render and never a frame-by-frame scrub. The whole story is one paused timeline with a label per stage; the scroll picks the label and the timeline glides to it at its natural speed, forwards or backwards, so scrolling back untypes the sentence and lifts the photograph back out. After the fifth answer, on the payoff beat, the wireframe builds itself into the finished site the way the hero's does, and holds there for as long as the visitor stays. Reduced motion and JavaScript off keep a server-rendered finished sketch, as the hero does.

## 5. Technique

**One timeline, seven labels, no callbacks.** `walkthrough-timeline.ts` builds a single paused `gsap.timeline` with labels `s0` to `s6`, every transition authored in place. State changes are all tweens on a stable DOM; the timeline contains no `call`, so it is fully reversible. `goTo(stage)` kills the scrub in flight and runs `tl.tweenTo(label, { duration, ease: 'none' })`, where `duration` is the natural distance capped by how many stops were crossed (`CONFIG.walkthrough.catchUp`: 1.4 s for one stop, plus 0.4 s per further stop, 2.4 s at most), so a visitor who flicks to the bottom watches the whole page assemble in one glide rather than waiting nine seconds. `ease: 'none'` keeps the authored curves inside each segment. GSAP core only; no ScrollTrigger, so `loadGsap` and ADR 0005's loader are untouched, and the chunk is the one the hero has already fetched.

**A stable DOM.** Today's `PhoneSketch` is drawn from a model and remounts per stage, which a reversible timeline cannot animate. The walkthrough gets its own phone, `walkthrough-frame.tsx`, drawn for this one brand with every state present at once: each part holds its slot and its answer as siblings (the dashed headline slot and the name; the hatch, the style gradient and the photograph; the grey button and its coloured twin stacked on top), and the timeline moves opacity and transforms between them. Colour is never tweened as a variable: every coloured element has a grey base and a coloured twin, so the colour beat is opacity only, compositor-cheap and reversible. The typing is one tiny tween per character on a proxy object, sequenced at `typingOffsets`' timings, so reverse deletes at the same rhythm.

**Layers, as the hero.** Inside the phone's screen: the wireframe layer in flow (it sets the height), the finished page absolute beneath it. `PhoneFrame`, `Bar`, `PhotoFill`, the hatch and `flipDelta` are reused. The FLIP build routine in `hero-loop.ts` (`addBuild`, `movesIn`, `zoomOf`) is extracted into a shared module parameterised by its beats table, with no change to the hero's behaviour; the hero's existing Playwright tests and a before-and-after contact sheet prove that.

**The scroll driver.** The three beats keep every word. Each `li` now carries the stages it paints (`data-stages="1 2"`, `"3"`, `"4 5 6"`) and a minimum height at `md` and up (about 60vh, 30vh and 90vh) so each stage owns roughly a third of a screen of scroll. A pure function `stageAt(anchorY, beats)` (unit-tested) gives the stage: each beat's stages trigger at even fractions of its height, and the stage is the highest whose line has passed the anchor. The anchor is the sticky frame's bottom edge plus a small margin, so a stage paints as its beat becomes readable beneath the frame on a phone and as it enters the lower third on desktop. One passive scroll listener, throttled to a frame, reads two rectangles; Lenis emits native scroll events, so nothing special is needed. Progress ("Question N of 5"), the segments and the caption are React state driven by the stop, not the timeline, so they are right even before GSAP arrives.

**Loading and rewinding.** The server renders the finished sketch (stage 5) in the wireframe layer, as today. A client that allows motion marks the wrapper `data-phase="empty"` in its first client render, and CSS shows the slots and hides the answers; that is the timeline's time-zero state, so when the timeline is built (once GSAP and the finished page's chunk have loaded and fonts are ready, triggered when the section is within a viewport of arriving) the attribute is removed in the same frame with no visible change, and the frame glides to whatever stop the visitor is already at. The finished page is a `next/dynamic` chunk with `ssr: false`, loaded only on motion clients, carrying its font and its photographs, exactly as `BuiltPage` is. A resize rebuilds the timeline and seeks to the current label.

**Below `md`.** The sticky block becomes the whole phone at zoom 1.15 (about 166 by 350 px) under the header, no mask, with the progress line above and the caption below, about 45vh in all, and the beats scroll beneath it with the same stage mapping. The typing types the whole sentence: the phone's paragraph shows two lines and the clamp hides the rest, as the hero's phone does.

## 8. Performance and budgets

- Scripts: the driver and the timeline are page code, about 3 KB gzipped; GSAP stays the lazy chunk it is. Budget 210,000 B, 205,956 measured.
- HTML: the wireframe layer holds every state, so it is heavier than today's stage-5 sketch, an estimated 1.5 to 2.5 KB gzipped more. The line is 38,000 B and 36,576 was measured on 5 September, so it is measured after the build and, if it crosses, raised to 40,000 in the ADR rather than trimming a state.
- Stylesheet: a few hundred bytes of utilities; 14,000 B line, 13,103 measured.
- Fonts and photographs: only for motion clients, fetched with the finished page's chunk when the section is a viewport away; one font file and four small WebPs (640 by 480 and three at 160 square).
- Paint: transforms and opacity throughout; the photograph's saturation is the one filter, as in the hero. Measured at 4x CPU throttle before sign-off, the way the hero was: no frame over 34 ms during any transition.
- LCP and CLS: the section is below the fold at every size; the sticky block's height is fixed by the frame, and the caption reserves the taller of its two lines.

## 10. Decisions for the owner

1. **The brand.** Fernbrook Gardens as above, or another name; the rest of the plan is unchanged by the name. Fonts, photographs and copy are all in one place each.
2. **Scroll-driven, as recommended, or a loop.** The alternative is the hero's mechanism: play the seven states on a timer whenever the section is on screen and loop. It is simpler, but it is a second autoplay loop on one page (WCAG 2.2.2 again, which the hero already knowingly fails), and it breaks the heading's promise that the visitor advances one question at a time. Scroll-driven motion moves only while the visitor moves, so 2.2.2 does not apply to it.
3. **The section gets taller.** About 2.2 screens of scroll on desktop and phone so each stage has room; the button lands about one screen lower than today. This is the price of scroll narration and the reason the current version cannot walk.
4. **Reduced motion and JavaScript off** see the finished sketch, as the hero's visitors do. They could instead see the finished site: that puts the font and the finished markup in the server HTML for everyone (about 3 KB and one 20 KB font file) and needs the HTML line raised. Not proposed.
5. **The label at the end.** "Question 5 of 5" stays over the finished site, all segments filled. It could instead read "Five answers in." from the build; one string and one test either way.
6. **Photographs.** Pexels placeholders, no people, chosen by us; the owner can swap any by replacing the file.

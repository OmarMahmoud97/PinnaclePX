## 1. What this plan is for

The page proves a mechanism and then stops. A visitor watches a wireframe fill in, learns the five questions are painless, is told the designs are a taster and the call is optional, and is asked to type. The marketing and business review of 5 September 2026 found eight gaps in what comes next: (1) no finished design appears anywhere, every visual is a sketch captioned "not one of the designs", and the page hides six real sites the studio has built while its hero paints one of those clients' briefs under a caption calling them "not a client"; (2) nothing says what a good site does for the owner; (3) nothing says what the real build includes, how long it takes, who writes the words, or who owns the site; (4) no visitor can recognise their own situation; (5) the page never addresses a DIY builder or the agency route; (6) nothing about hosting, updates or care after launch; (7) three new objections are unanswered (training, ownership, "can I see an example first?"); (8) the only exits are to start or to book a call.

Two things are excluded by the owner and this plan does not depend on them: a named person, founder bio or photograph, studio town or contact email (`SITE.town` and `SITE.contactEmail` stay `null`); and legal footer content, SEO or discovery work (the example routes stay `noindex`).

**The thesis.** Close the loop the hero opens, with the same brief, which is a client's. VetPres already fills the hero sketch and the How it works phone. Let that brief become a real Aurora render in the frames the page already owns, labelled as the five-minute version, and put it beside the site the studio actually designed for VetPres after a conversation (One client, start to finish, section 3.5); say what a real site does for an owner like the reader and let them recognise themselves (Four things your site has to do); make the ask (Taster), then show what the ask produced six times over: the six sites the studio built for the six businesses who came to it through this site, each a dated capture with the client's name and trade, linking to the site itself (Our work, section 3.7b); state the paid step as a numbered process that runs past launch (If you like one, here is what happens next, with After launch inside); compare the two ways of getting a site on checkable facts (Doing it yourself, or asking us); and give the not-ready visitor two exits that cost nothing (see the sites we have built; send this page). Five new sections, one thread. Nothing invented and no sentence saying so (the dated captures, the printed addresses and the permission line are the proof; a denial beside them subtracts), nothing that is not built, no client named or shown without their written permission, and no taster render ever presented as a client's site. Every new claim is gated on the template count, a recorded consent or an owner decision, and a claim whose facts are unconfirmed is omitted, not softened.

Vocabulary, fixed once and used everywhere below (and proposed for `PRODUCT.md`'s Terminology line in section 5): **a client's brief** for the sentence the sketch paints; **the five-minute version** for a taster render of a client's brief; **the site we designed for them** for a client's real site; **a client of ours** as the only relationship word; **Our work** for the six-site band. Never "example brief", "example business" or "not a client" about any of the six; never "trusted by", "clients include" or "worked with". On the invented-business point, `docs/home-page-plan.md` 3.5, `docs/home-page-design-plan.md` 3.5, 4 (Source 2), 11.3 and line 67, ADR 0006 line 18, `docs/hero-loop-plan.md:103,106` and the eight sweeps where they say "no client work" are superseded by the owner's correction and are not reopened otherwise; their premise came from `PRODUCT.md` line 47, which section 5 proposes replacing.

## 3. Section by section

Conventions for every new section, stated once. Recipes are the classes in `app/_components/section-styles.ts` and `components/ui/*.ts` (`codebase-map.md` 1.2 to 1.5, **observed**). Every H2 uses `titleHeading` and ends with a full stop; every card or step title `cardHeading` and never a stop; every lead `text-lead text-pretty text-on-surface-muted`; every label, count, date and honesty line `captionStyles` (mono), never persuasion. Every list container carries `data-reveal` with `revealDelay(index)` on its direct children, which gives the existing fade-and-rise under motion, a fade under reduced motion and plain visibility with JavaScript off; no new keyframes anywhere, so `e2e/reduced-motion.spec.ts` changes only its scroll list: `examples` and `work` join it in the change that ships each band (today it scrolls `how-it-works`, `outcomes`, `taster`, `real-build`, `your-options`, `straight-answers`, `faq` and `cta`, **observed**, so only the two bands' reveals would otherwise never be exercised under reduced motion). Every phone layout is the tiered stack (heading column `max-md:pb-3`, body `max-md:pt-6`, 24 px gutter) and `scrollWidth === 390` holds. Nothing new says "AI", "generate", "generated", "instantly", "live" about a design or a site ("as it looked on {date}" does that job), "copy", "guarantee", "risk-free", "unlimited" or "24/7"; no template's name (Aurora) appears anywhere on the home page, because a named layout reads to a burned buyer as the template product they were sold last time (the name lives in the route path and the `template` event property only). Every new `<Link>` to a hash of the page glides through Lenis with no code (ADR 0021, **observed**). ### 4.2 The render pipeline decision

Static captures, not a live embed and not an iframe. Aurora lays out by viewport (32 `md:` utilities and six `vh` values, zero container queries) and emits `id="main"`, `#how-it-works` and a second `<h1>`, so a scaled inline render lays out wrong and collides with the page; one live instance is 4.4 KB gzipped of HTML against 3.4 KB of headroom (`codebase-map.md` 6.4, **observed**). An iframe is a second document, a keyboard-trap risk, and unscanned by axe; static captures of the same route are the facade web.dev recommends (**evidence**, `gallery-patterns.md` 1.3 to 1.4). Write t02 onwards with container queries and add the CI grep for `md:`, `lg:`, `vh`, `dvh`, `svh` under `templates/` (decision 24) so a live render of one hero example stays possible later; refactor Aurora only when that is wanted.

`scripts/render-examples.mts` (`pnpm render:examples`, which runs `node --experimental-strip-types scripts/render-examples.mts`: `engines` is `>=22.12`, type stripping is flagged on 22.12 to 22.17 and unflagged from 22.18 and on the installed 24.12, **observed**; imports are relative, `../lib/config.ts`, because `@/` aliases do not resolve outside the bundler), run by hand against `pnpm build && pnpm start` with the `ci.yml` placeholder env (the example route touches no service), or as a manually triggered Actions job on `ubuntu-latest` where `ci.yml` already installs Chromium; the Vercel build image has no browser (**observed**). Playwright 1.62.1 and `sharp` 0.35.4 are installed, so no new dependency. For every ready template and every client with a fixture: `devices['iPhone 13']` at `deviceScaleFactor: 2` (and, from state two, a 1440x900 context at `deviceScaleFactor: 1` if a desktop capture of a first look is ever shown); `reducedMotion: 'reduce'`; wait for `document.fonts.ready`; `screenshot({ animations: 'disabled', scale: 'device', type: 'png' })` of the first viewport; the phone capture cropped to 9:19; then `sharp` resizes to the one width the page shows (phone 432, 2x of 216 px; no 648 file, section 3.5) and encodes it as AVIF and WebP, committed under `app/_images/examples/{client}-{template}-phone-432.{avif,webp}`. The caps in `CONFIG.examples` are set from that first render and committed with the captures (something like the 432 phone file under 40 KB; a raw phone capture of a lit hero with a photograph would not meet a cap guessed lower, so a cap written before the render fails on its first run by construction); after that the script fails when a file exceeds its cap. A sidecar `manifest.json` records, per capture, the viewport, `renderedAt`, `clientSignOff` (the date the client saw the frozen run), the list of hashed paths and a hash over them: `templates/{template}/**`, the fixture, `app/globals.css` (type scale, tokens, motion), `lib/tokens/**` (the derived token set), `app/preview/_components/fonts.ts` (the VetPres type pair) and `app/examples/[client]/[template]/page.tsx`, because a change to any of these changes the picture. A Vitest test (`tests/integration/examples-manifest.test.ts`) reads the path list from the manifest, recomputes each hash and fails when a committed capture is older than anything it depends on; no pixel diff in CI, because Windows and Linux antialias differently and the diff would be noise (**practitioner opinion**). The band's caption date is read from the manifest, so the picture and its label can never drift apart.

### 4.3 Capturing the six client sites (new)

The five-minute captures come from a route this repository owns, so a source hash can prove the picture is current. A client's site is not in this repository, so nothing here can prove the picture matches the site today; the controls are a dated caption, a consent that covers the capture, a scheduled check that watches for drift and tells a person, an archive snapshot as evidence, and a manifest that decides what is shown.

**One script, two modes.** Client capture is a second mode of the same script (`pnpm capture:clients`, which runs `node --experimental-strip-types scripts/render-examples.mts --clients`), because the encode step, the frame widths and the manifest shape are shared and a second script would drift from the first. Nothing new is installed (Playwright 1.62.1 and `sharp` 0.35.4, **observed**). A prototype of this mode ran all six sites today in about 2.5 minutes outside the repo (`scratchpad/proof-research/capture.mjs`, **observed**); the repo version adds consent gating, overlay handling and the manifest fields below. It runs by hand on the owner's machine or as a manually dispatched Actions job on `ubuntu-latest`; never in the build.

**Consent is the authorisation.** `robots.txt` governs crawlers that recursively traverse links and its rules "are not a form of access authorization" (RFC 9309, **evidence**); one scripted screenshot of one consented URL every quarter is not crawling. The script reads the file anyway and records the result. The client's written consent says in plain words that the studio may load their site with an automated browser to take the picture (section 7.1's email). The script identifies itself (`PinnaclePX-capture/1.0 (+{NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL}/#work)`) so the client's logs show what happened, never uses stealth tooling to pass bot protection (ask the client or host to allow the user agent instead), and refuses to run for a client whose `consent.on` is missing or later than today, whose `scope` lacks `'capture'`, or whose `status` is not `shown` (**business reasoning**, `consent-and-cap.md` section 4). Input is `CLIENT_ITEMS` in `app/_components/client-items.ts`, one row per client:

```ts
{
  slug: 'vetpres',
  name: 'VetPres',
  trade: 'Veterinary prescription software',   // the owner writes, the client approves in the consent
  place: null,
  need: null,                                   // optional, agreed in the consent (decision 44)
  url: 'https://vetpres.com/',
  consent: { on: '2026-09-08', id: 'consent-2026-09-vetpres', scope: ['name', 'capture', 'link', 'rerun'] },
  status: 'shown',                              // 'shown' | 'changed' | 'offline' | 'withdrawn'
  order: 2,
  desktop: true,                                // the journey client only
  firstContactWasTheSite: true,                 // mirrors register field 12; the register test checks they agree
  preLinkCheck: 'pass',                         // 'pass' | 'fail' | null; a card links only on 'pass' (decision 50)
}
```

The consent documents never enter the public repository (the repo is public, push protection on); `consent.id` names a file in the owner's private store (section 7.1). The banner and widget selectors the script uses are a constant in `scripts/`, not in `CLIENT_ITEMS` (which joins `COPY`).

**Capture.** For each shown row: `devices['iPhone 13']` at `deviceScaleFactor: 2` and, for rows with `desktop: true`, a 1440x900 context at `deviceScaleFactor: 1`, both `reducedMotion: 'reduce'`, `locale: 'en-GB'`; `goto` with `domcontentloaded`, then `networkidle` capped at 20 s, `document.fonts.ready`, a 1.2 s settle; a banner pass that clicks the first visible button matching the accept regex (Termly's "Accept" was needed on WithU and Urunn today, **observed**) and records `bannerDismissed`; `page.evaluate` pauses every `<video>` so the poster or a fixed frame is captured (Mvmnt and Urunn autoplay hero video that `reducedMotion` does not stop, **observed**); the screenshot `style` option hides overlay chrome only (chat bubbles on Mvmnt and WithU, cookie components) and records the selector list as `overlaysHidden`; then `screenshot({ animations: 'disabled', scale: 'device', type: 'png' })` of the first viewport only. Nothing else is allowed to change the picture: no hidden content elements, no compositing, no retouching, no blur, never a face retouched (CAP 3.1, **evidence**). Live sites are not deterministic (a slideshow, a random quote), so the script captures twice, warns when the two `textHash`es differ, and the owner picks the frame that represents the site. Then `sharp` crops the phone PNG to 9:19 from the top, resizes to 432, resizes the desktop PNG to 672 and 1344 (the journey client only), encodes AVIF and WebP, and fails over the `CONFIG.work` caps.

**`app/_images/work/manifest.json`**, per client: `slug`, `sourceUrl`, `finalUrl` (after redirects; `withutraining.com` and `withu.fit` redirect to `withuapp.com`, `urunn.com` to `www.urunn.com`, **observed**), `httpStatus`, `capturedAt` (ISO; the caption renders the date in full), `viewport`, `deviceScaleFactor`, `pageSignature: { title, h1, textHash }` (sha256 of the first screen's `innerText`), `robots`, `bannerDismissed`, `overlaysHidden`, `videoPaused`, `consentOn`, `consentId` (the register's opaque id, never a name or email), `waybackUrl`, `status: 'shown' | 'changed' | 'offline' | 'withdrawn'`, `lastCheckedAt`, and the file list with `sha256` and bytes. The band renders only `status: 'shown'`; the caption date and domain render from `capturedAt` and `finalUrl`, so the picture and its label cannot drift apart; every count on the page comes from the shown set or the built list as section 3.11 says. No source hash is possible for a site the repo does not own; `textHash` and `h1` are the drift signal instead.

**Documentary evidence.** At each capture the script asks the Wayback Machine to save the URL (Save Page Now; the SPN2 API's parameters are **Unverified**, not read at source today, so read the docs before the script depends on them; if the call fails the capture still commits and the owner saves the page by hand) and records the snapshot URL in the manifest and the register. It is the studio's proof, independent of its own files, of what was live on the caption's date (CAP 3.7 and the ASA's "signed and dated proof" standard for before-and-after imagery, **evidence**).

**Caps.** `CONFIG.work.maxPhoneBytes` and `maxDesktopBytes`, set from the first committed encode and never guessed (today's largest 432 phone file is VetPres's 37,950 B WebP; the largest 1344 desktop file Go Wild's 99,818 B WebP, **observed**), `staleDays: 90` and `staleFailDays: 180`. The script fails over cap.

**Cadence and drift.** Re-capture every 90 days, on any drift alert, and whenever a client says their site changed; 90 days keeps "as it looked on {date}" within a season, and a longer gap makes the date read as a confession rather than a reassurance (Stanford guideline 8, **evidence**; no studio observed today dates a capture at all, `portfolio-patterns.md` 5.1). `.github/workflows/client-drift.yml` runs weekly on `ubuntu-latest`: for each shown client it fetches `sourceUrl` and compares HTTP status, the `finalUrl` host, `<title>`, H1 and `textHash` with `pageSignature`; a non-200 in two consecutive weeks, a redirect to another host, or a changed signature opens one issue per client and changes nothing by itself. It never commits a capture and never flips a status, because a parked, hijacked or redesigned page would otherwise land on the studio's home page unseen (**practitioner opinion**). A changed H1 is a strong sign of a redesign or a new owner; a changed title alone is often a tagline edit. `tests/integration/work-manifest.test.ts`: every shown client has a manifest entry with `consentOn` on or before `capturedAt`; every referenced file exists with the recorded hash and is under its cap; every `sourceUrl` is `https:` and every `finalUrl` host equals the rendered link host; the caption date equals `capturedAt`; a capture older than `staleDays` warns and older than `staleFailDays` fails, so a forgotten re-capture becomes a red build before the caption is half a year old without turning an unrelated PR red on day 91. No pixel diff in CI.

**When a site changes, goes down, or a client withdraws** (decision 45).

- Changed by the studio (a later iteration): re-capture at the next run; the caption date moves; the standing consent clause covers it.
- Changed by someone else (the client rebuilt elsewhere): the picture is the studio's work and the link is not, so the card leaves the band (`status: 'changed'`); the register keeps the capture and the Wayback snapshot; the footnote count drops; About's count is unchanged (it counts sites built). Showing "designed and built by us" over a link to another studio's work would be the omission CAP 3.3 forbids. The non-default alternative, kept for the owner: keep the card with the caption > {Client}'s site as we built it, {date}. Their site has changed since.
  and no link; it is honest but keeps a picture of work the reader cannot check, so removal is the default (CAP 3.1 and 3.3, **business reasoning**).
- Offline (two weekly checks fail, or the domain changes hands): `status: 'offline'`; the card leaves the band; if the site returns unchanged, a fresh capture returns it; after 30 days down or a change of ownership the owner asks the client before keeping them anywhere.
- Withdrawn (the client emails): `status: 'withdrawn'` the same day; the files, the imports and the row's name, slug, URL and trade go in one commit, leaving only `{ consent: { id }, status: 'withdrawn' }` so About's built count holds while no name, slug or address renders in a public file; redeploy; the About name leaves the line; the count follows the consent's clause (the anonymous count may keep them unless they ask otherwise). The consent fixes the removal period at five working days from the client's email so nobody argues about "reasonable notice" (UK GDPR Art 17's "without undue delay" where a person's image is the reason, **evidence**), and the studio aims for the same day; Vercel serves the new build at once, and hashed static assets already cached by browsers, edge nodes, search caches and web archives are outside the studio's control, which the consent says plainly. The removal commit and date go in the register row; the row and the private file are kept for the life of the display plus six years (Limitation Act 1980 s5, **evidence**). If the withdrawing client is the flagship (VetPres), the hero's brief, the illustration and the journey are re-authored for the second consented client (decision 46): `example-brief.ts`, `built-copy.ts`, `photos.ts` and `demo.typing` are hand-tuned to VetPres's 230-character sentence, so this is a content rebuild, not a config switch, and Go Wild's version is prepared in advance only if the owner wants the fallback ready.
- Never: a grey placeholder card, a "coming back soon" tile, or a capture with no link and no explanation.

Copyright: CDPA s11 makes the studio first owner, but a paying client may hold an implied licence or equitable title (_Robin Ray v Classic FM_, _Griggs v Evans_, **evidence** via summaries) and decision 7 assigns the site on final payment, so the client's written licence, not the studio's authorship, is the basis for showing it (`consent-and-cap.md` 1.1). Trade marks: a capture of the client's own site with a factual label is referential and descriptive use under TMA s11(2)(b) and (c) subject to honest practices (**evidence**); a bare logo strip would be the weaker case and is not built.

## 10. Risks, and what was deliberately left out

Risks:

1. **Length.** About 21 phone screens for a page whose median sentence is six words. Controls: the ten-screen cap on the additions, the written cut order in 2.3, and the sequential tests in item 22. If Your options does not move starts, it becomes one FAQ item. The two proof bands are never the cut.
2. **The one-template contradiction.** The page promises three designs and builds one today. The journey never contradicts the offer (it says "our first layout", never "one design per visitor"), but a visitor who submits today gets one. Decision 19's launch gate is the honest fix short of rewording fourteen sentences.
3. **Contract terms on a marketing page.** Every gated line in The real build, Your options and the FAQ becomes a contract term for a consumer client. Controls: the claims register, "contract first, page second", and the rule that an unconfirmed step is omitted. A one-person studio's holiday week is the test every timeline and reply-time line must pass.
4. **Comparison compliance.** The builder column is a comparison with identifiable competitors. Controls: verifiable facts only, the signpost, no quality words, the hedge on row 5, the generous line, a re-check of the left column against Wix's and Squarespace's own pages at each review, recorded in the register. If the ASA's advice hardens, the section collapses to an FAQ item without losing the page.
5. **Stale pictures.** A template change that is not re-rendered leaves a picture of something the route no longer shows; the examples manifest test fails CI and the render job is one command. A client's site can change without any file in this repository changing, so no hash can catch it; the controls are the dated caption (true on every later day), the 90-day cadence with a warning at 90 days and a failure at 180, the weekly drift job that opens an issue, the archive snapshot as the studio's proof of what was live, and the client's word as the trigger. A link that 404s is a Stanford guideline 10 failure on this page even though the fault is elsewhere, which is why offline cards leave the band.
6. **The example's wording.** If the pipeline's VetPres draft reads badly, the temptation is to hand-fix it and keep "what came back". Do not: freeze what came back and let FAQ 9 ("What if the wording is wrong?") carry it, or hand-write it and say "what it looks like". The draft now sits under a real client's name: VetPres sees the frozen five-minute version before publication and the consent names that capture; a bad draft is withdrawn from the page, never hand-fixed and kept as "came back".
7. **Budgets.** The stylesheet and HTML increases are certain, not a risk to watch: on the 5 September production build the headroom is 549 B of CSS (13,451 of 14,000) and 3,191 B of HTML (21,809 of 25,000), and five sections, seventeen FAQ items, six build steps, seven comparison rows and six cards cannot fit in either (**observed**). The script budget holds because neither band adds client code (`<picture>`, not `next/image`; the share leaf is the one new client file). Roadmap item 9 measures after items 4 to 6 and writes the ADR before items 14 and 14b; prefer existing utilities (the call agenda's `flex gap-3` and `w-[9ch] shrink-0`, the Taster's step recipe, the What you get cells) to new arbitrary values so the CSS growth is labels and copy. Never trim a label to make a number.
8. **Vercel Hobby.** Non-commercial, and it records no custom event, so section 9 measures nothing until Pro (decision 29); Plus if the share source is to be read.
9. **The share control as a leak.** It sends nothing to the studio and captures nothing; the only data is `share_click` and the `utm_source` on the return visit. Keep it that way; a "send to a friend" form would need a privacy-page change and would breach "No sign-up".
10. **Nav width at 768 px** (**Unverified**). The tablet project measures it; decision 20 says which link yields.
11. **The training answer ages.** Both Anthropic pages were read on 5 September 2026. The Covered Models rule (Claude Fable 5 and 5.1, policy effective 9 June 2026) keeps the same 30-day period and only removes the zero-data-retention option, so a model change onto one does not falsify FAQ 7; the pipeline today runs `claude-sonnet-5` and `claude-haiku-4-5` (`lib/config.ts`, **observed**), neither a Covered Model. Re-read both pages at every model change anyway, and keep "normally" for the flagged-content exception (up to two years). The register's check date is the control.
12. **The privacy page's dangling contact.** FAQ 6 and 7 now link to `/privacy`, which says "Email us at the address on the home page" while no address exists. Decision 15 lands before item 8 ships.
13. **Consent that never arrives.** The hero, How it works and the journey all name VetPres. If VetPres declines, the unnamed captions stay, the journey ships only once a second client has consented and its brief, illustration and photograph have been re-authored (decision 46), and VetPres's sentence leaves `example-brief.ts` for that client's; if fewer than three clients consent, Our work does not ship and About carries the count line only, with no names. Nothing waits on a consent to be honest; only the naming does.
14. **The linked sites are judged as the studio's work, because they are.** VetPres is blank without JavaScript and shows a 2023 copyright and a waitlist; Go Wild compiles JSX in the browser from a CDN (**observed**). A prospect who opens either on a slow phone judges the studio by it, and the first site the sceptical reader taps is the journey's. Controls: decision 50's pre-link check per site as a pass or fail, recorded in the register and the row; no link and no card until a pass; the journey led by the first client that passes; the two questions to VetPres; the studio's own repair where it manages the site; never a hidden link, a caveat or a flattering crop. The cost of the gate is real: if neither VetPres nor Go Wild can be made to pass, the proof chain waits, and that is the right order (a link to the studio's weakest evidence is worse than no link).
15. **Four of six are one group.** A reader who works it out alone reads "six businesses" as a stretch; the corporate history is **Unverified**. Controls: decision 44's plain line, the cards' own trade lines, the rule that the page never says "unrelated", and the rule that the four ship only once the owner has confirmed in writing what the studio did on each.
16. **The count outliving the fact.** A seventh client, a withdrawal, a referral: each changes a sentence on the page. Control: every count and name renders from `CLIENT_ITEMS` and the manifest, About's count from the built list and the H2's from the shown set, and the register test ties them together.
17. **The public repository.** `docs/claims-register.md`, `client-items.ts` and the work manifest are public. They hold trading names, URLs, dates, scopes and opaque consent ids only; the person's name, email and the consent itself live outside the repo (UK GDPR Art 4(1), **evidence**), and a withdrawn row keeps no name, slug or URL. Push protection is on.

# PinnaclePX home page design research

Prepared 3 September 2026. The evidence behind the next revision of `docs/home-page-plan.md`. Six sweeps (studios, imagery, motion, GSAP, copy, performance) were run and the two technical sweeps were re-checked by independent verifiers; where a verifier corrected a claim the corrected version is used and marked **Correction**. Where a verifier could not confirm a claim it is marked **Unverified**. Evidence and practitioner opinion are marked on every claim.

## 1. The diagnosis in one paragraph

The page is honest, fast and text-only, and to a wary owner that combination reads as "a studio with nothing to show". Baymard's SaaS testing found that real UI visuals near the top of the home page "quickly engaged participants and encouraged deeper exploration" and that actual screenshots beat graphical representations (evidence, https://baymard.com/blog/saas-website-ux-best-practices); Harry Dry's test for every hero is "show me your product, or even better, your product in action" (practitioner, https://marketingexamples.com/conversion/landing-page-guide); the page shows neither, so the reader has to take four icon cards and nine FAQ answers on trust. The absence of motion is not the problem: the page has zero autonomous movement, which every source below endorses, and its one real defect there is a reduced-motion rule that flattens even harmless fades. What is missing is the one thing the studio already owns, the live sketch on `/start`, a wireframe that paints itself from the visitor's answers. It is information-carrying imagery in NN/g's sense (evidence, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/), it is the product working rather than a picture of it, and it is schematic enough that a sceptic cannot read it as a faked design. The fix is to bring the mechanism onto the home page (a server-rendered example sketch now, the visitor's own typing later), to manufacture the examples gallery from the product when three templates render, to tighten a handful of copy lines that currently contradict each other, and to write down a motion, imagery and performance budget before any of it is built.

## 2. What the best studios do

Every site below was read as markup on 3 September 2026. **Unverified:** motion could not be observed directly (the fetch reads HTML, not animation), so the motion column rests on Awwwards feature tags, published case studies and the sites' own copy. A human should open Malvah, Locomotive, Studio Freight, Sennep, MadeByShape and Fit Design at 390 px before copying anything.

### The pattern

- Award sites are Experience mode and pay for it in usability. The Awwwards Sites of the Year 2025 are a WebGL delivery game (Messenger) and a Formula 1 driver site (Lando Norris) (observed, https://www.awwwards.com/websites/sites_of_the_year/). On every awarded studio site checked, the jury's usability sub-score is the lowest of the four: Revelatio 7.19 against design 7.45, No Art 7.16 against creativity 7.36, Prødux 7.1 against 7.52, basement 7.09 against 7.97, Unseen 7.57 against 8.4 (observed, https://www.awwwards.com/sites/revelatio-studio). Preloaders, entry gates, sound and cursor effects are optimised for a jury, not for a physio owner deciding whether to type a sentence.
- The best studios state restraint as a principle. Locomotive's own case study: "We avoided excessive animations and gadgets to convey only the essentials" and one transition idea (letter shuffle) reused everywhere (practitioner, https://www.awwwards.com/case-study-reinventing-locomotive-r.html). Studio Freight's principle is "brutal elegance ... no more and no less" (practitioner, https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/29/studio-freight-moving-missions-forward/).
- Pages that ask for a decision put the real mechanism in the hero and proof below it. Linear's hero is actual application UI, Framer's is the live product working, and both push testimonials and logos below the fold (observed, https://linear.app/, https://www.framer.com/). Significa puts "Get a quote" in the header and its process as five verbs in the H1 (observed, https://significa.co/).
- Small studios earn trust with a name, a place, an email and a reply-time promise. Malvah (a headline, a strip of six real pieces, a team photo, a Cape Town address, no client logos; observed, https://malvah.co/), Sennep (named founders, a Design Week interview; observed, https://www.sennep.com/), Appero (one person, three steps, "replies within 24 hours", a jargon translator, no logos or reviews; observed, https://appero.studio/), Designjoy ("run entirely by Brett"; observed, https://www.designjoy.co/). **Unverified:** Malvah as Awwwards Studio of the Year 2025 is confirmed only by Malvah's own headline and an LBBOnline headline; the Awwwards annual page returned 404.
- The "see it before you commit" promise is not unique in the UK. Fit Design (London) already sells "Request Homepage Preview" with "No commitment. See the potential." and tiers from £5,000 (observed, https://www.fitdesignldn.com/). **Unverified:** the "48 hours, clickable prototype, strategy session" copy attributed to Fit Design comes from a search snippet; the page returned 404. PinnaclePX must therefore make its difference legible: three designs, about five minutes, in your own brand, nobody drawing it by hand, nobody calling.
- Price in the headline reads budget-tier. Appero ("from £499") and Bemunchie put price first and read as the cheap end (observed). This supports the no-price rule.

### Anti-patterns confirmed across the sweep

Preloaders, curtains and entry gates (NN/g: no indicator at all under one second; evidence, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators/); autoplay hero video or auto-rotating carousels (NN/g: "do not auto-forward on mobile"; evidence, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/designing-effective-carousels/; Baymard: 46% of home page carousels have usability issues, evidence, https://baymard.com/blog/homepage-carousel); scroll-reveal on every section, pinned sections, parallax (NN/g scrolljacking study: most participants disoriented, a prospect would "just move on"; evidence, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/); cursor followers and hover-only reveals (invisible to the 83% of visits on mobile; evidence, https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/); Liquid Glass blur cards, purple gradients, cyan-on-dark and coloured glows on dark (the most recognised 2026 template tells; practitioner, https://impeccable.style/slop/ and https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/ai-design-slop-and-how-to-spot-it); logo walls and star ratings without names; enterprise studio-speak ("ambitious ideas for ambitious brands"); identical icon-card grids and tiny 01/02/03 labels, both of which the current page still has.

## 3. Imagery strategy

### Manufacture from the product

1. **The live sketch as the hero visual, now.** Move `brief-sketch.tsx`, `phone-sketch.tsx`, `sketch-parts.tsx` and `sketch-model.ts` from `app/start/_components` to `components/sketch/` so `app/_components` may import them under the ESLint boundaries, and render them on the server with a fixture model from `lib/brief/examples.ts` (one of the three demo briefs, wordmark fallback, palette colour, stage 5). None of the sketch components carries `'use client'`, so the hero ships zero extra JavaScript and the LCP element stays the H1 (observed, `app/start/_components/brief-sketch.tsx`; official-docs, https://web.dev/articles/optimize-lcp). Keep the `aria-hidden` plus `sr-only` sentence pattern from `SketchChips`. Caption: "An example brief. Yours paints in as you answer." Navattic's 2026 report found 86% of top demo captures are live HTML and that interactive demos convert 12% higher than video (evidence, https://www.navattic.com/report/state-of-the-interactive-product-demo-2026). Two-column at `lg`, H1 left, sketch right.
2. **A colour re-tint strip under the sketch.** Five palette chips (buttons, `aria-pressed` on the current one) that set the `--sketch-*` variables on the wrapper. `lib/brief/sketch.ts` already derives every accent from one colour through oklch relative colour clamped to carry white text, which is the exact claim the What you get card makes; a chip turns the claim into a demonstration, user-initiated and under NN/g's 0.1 s feedback bar (evidence, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-usability/). One tiny client leaf; default state server-rendered so nothing flashes.
3. **The examples gallery, rendered by Playwright, when t01 to t03 and `/preview/[slug]` exist.** `scripts/render-examples.ts` visits a `noindex` route per fixture and template, captures at 1440x900 with `deviceScaleFactor: 2`, `scale: 'device'`, `animations: 'disabled'`, `reducedMotion: 'reduce'`, clips the hero band for the card, and takes a phone crop with `devices['iPhone 13']` (official-docs, https://playwright.dev/docs/emulation; Playwright 1.62.1 is already installed, no new dependency). Commit PNGs under `app/_components/examples/` and import them statically so `next/image` supplies width, height and `blurDataURL` and caches them immutably; `public/` is served with `max-age=0` (official-docs, `node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/03-api-reference/02-components/image.md`). Set `sizes` on every card, add `images.formats: ['image/avif', 'image/webp']` (AVIF is 20% smaller for 50% more encode time, cached after first request). Re-run the script in CI on any change under `templates/` and fail on a git diff. Card: hero crop, phone crop, business name, the one-line brief that produced it, the label, a link to the live preview. Static grid or tablist, never a rotator.
4. **A before-and-after pair in How it works** once one template renders: the sketch at stage 5 on the left, the rendered design of the same brief on the right, labelled "What you see while you answer" and "What comes back". Identical input, visible transformation; the strongest of the three fixtures only.
5. **`opengraph-image.tsx`** can draw a simplified flexbox-only sketch; Satori supports no grid, no `calc()`, no external stylesheets (official-docs, https://github.com/vercel/satori). It cannot render the Tailwind templates; Playwright does that.

### Two decisions before t01 is written

- **Templates lay out by container queries**, `@container`, `@md:`, `cqi`, never `md:` or `vw`/`vh`. Tailwind v4 ships container variants in core (official-docs, https://tailwindcss.com/docs/responsive-design). A template written with viewport breakpoints renders its mobile layout inside any home-page frame under 768 px; a container-query template is a desktop inside a browser frame, a phone inside a phone frame, and full width on `/preview`, and it can be shown live on the home page as `inert` DOM (Baseline April 2023; official-docs, https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Global_attributes/inert) scaled with CSS `zoom` (Baseline May 2024, affects layout unlike `transform: scale`; official-docs, https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/zoom) instead of nine iframes, each of which costs its own document and script (official-docs, https://web.dev/articles/iframe-lazy-loading). Add a CI grep for `md:`, `lg:` and `vh` under `templates/`.
- **The Vercel plan.** Hobby is "restricted to non-commercial personal use only" and caps image transformations at 5,000 a month, after which images return 402 and alt text shows (official-docs, https://vercel.com/docs/image-optimization/limits-and-pricing). Nine thumbnails times eight widths times two formats is about 150 transformations, trivial; the plan tier is the real question for a commercial studio.

### What the owner must supply

Three example briefs written as a real owner would (five answers each); a decision on fixture logos (the wordmark fallback is honest and needs nothing); real photographs of places, animals and objects if available (PRODUCT.md line 44 says the owner can supply them; if people appear, the no-people rule must be agreed to cover owner-supplied images too); sign-off on the label wording; a Companies House and trade mark check on Mvmnt, VetPres and Go Wild Dog Walking so no real trader appears to be a client.

### Honesty labels

CAP Code 3.1 (must not materially mislead), 3.3 (must not omit material information) and 3.7 (hold evidence for objective claims) apply (official-docs, https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html), and PRODUCT.md line 45 already rules that example businesses show only when labelled. Card label: "Example brief, not a client." Preview page line: "Made from a five-answer brief we wrote ourselves. No client was involved." Show the five answers beside the designs. The no-founder-photo rule is supported by evidence, not preference: in CXL's 445-participant study, agency home pages without a founder photo scored higher on professionalism on all eight sites tested, significantly on three (evidence, read through a text proxy because the page returned 403, https://cxl.com/research-study/appeal-to-authority-credibility/). Pexels imagery for demo slots is licensed for commercial use without attribution but must not imply endorsement or show identifiable people badly (official-docs, https://www.pexels.com/license/); filter for no people.

## 4. Motion and interaction principles

### The rules

1. **Nothing moves until the visitor acts.** No reveal-on-scroll, parallax, marquee, pulsing dot, blinking cursor, autoplay or auto-cycling. Motion in peripheral vision triggers an involuntary attention shift and irrelevant motion "can substantially degrade the user experience" (practitioner, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-purpose-ux/); a Siemens test user missed a deal "in 98-point font" because the carousel rotated (evidence, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/auto-forwarding/); Notre Dame's carousel data shows about 1% of visitors click a feature, 84% of them on slide one (evidence, https://erikrunyon.com/2013/01/carousel-interaction-stats/). WCAG 2.2.2 (Level A) requires a pause control when moving content starts automatically, lasts more than five seconds and sits beside other content (official-docs, https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html); a static page never needs the control.
2. **One authored moment, and it is feedback.** The only motion category every source endorses is a response to the visitor's own input (NN/g "feedback and state change"; Rauno Freiberg "responsive gestures", practitioner, https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design; Calabro's functional versus extraneous split, practitioner, https://trevorcalabro.substack.com/p/most-ui-animations-shouldnt-exist). For this page that is the sketch painting as the visitor types (phase C, when the hero field posts somewhere real) and, later, a React ViewTransition morph from the hero sketch into the `/start` sketch on the CTA click.
3. **Under 300 ms, ease-out on entry, transform and opacity only.** Emil Kowalski: "usually shorter than 300ms", ease-out because "it starts fast and slows down at the end", never animate padding or margin (practitioner, https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations); his published standards: press 100 to 160 ms at `scale(0.97)`, stagger 30 to 80 ms, never ease-in on UI, "reduced motion means fewer and gentler animations, not zero" (practitioner, https://github.com/emilkowalski/skills/blob/main/skills/review-animations/STANDARDS.md). NN/g: 100 ms for small feedback, 200 to 300 ms for substantial changes, 500 ms "starts to feel like a real drag" (practitioner, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/). Rauno's checklist: interaction feedback at or under 200 ms, motion proportional to trigger size, no hover on touch, no font-weight change on hover, loops pause off screen (practitioner, https://interfaces.rauno.me/). **Unverified:** Apple HIG ("prefer quick, precise animations") was read through search snippets, and Material 3's tokens (short1 50 ms to extralong4 1000 ms, standard `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`) were read from Flutter's `motion.dart` because both pages render client-side.
4. **No hover lift, no shadow change, no bounce.** Moving an element out from under the cursor causes hover flicker; press states scale toward the cursor and cannot (practitioner, https://www.joshwcomeau.com/animation/css-transitions/). Shadows and blur are paint-expensive (official-docs, https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide). Bounce and elastic easing, image hover transforms and marquees are on the 2026 template-tell lists.
5. **Reduce, do not remove.** WCAG 2.3.3 (Level AAA, so beyond the AA target but cheap to honour) defines motion animation so that colour and opacity changes are excluded (official-docs, https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html). **Correction:** a WCAG 2.2 erratum dated 27 June 2025 removed blurring from the exclusion, so blur changes may now count as motion; only colour and opacity are safe under reduce (official-docs, https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/errata/). **Correction:** web.dev's prefers-reduced-motion article does not advise toning motion down or swapping slides for fades; its demo removes reveal animations outright and its one transferable suggestion is loading animation CSS under `media="(prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)"` (official-docs, https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion). The "keep crossfades, drop positional movement" advice comes from the Next 16 view-transitions guide and from Josh Comeau (practitioner, https://www.joshwcomeau.com/react/prefers-reduced-motion/). The current `globals.css` block sets every duration to 0.01 ms with `!important`, which also cancels the sketch's colour re-tint, which is feedback, not motion.
6. **Judge every motion on a phone.** 83% of landing page visits are mobile (evidence, Unbounce). Tailwind 4's `hover` variant is already wrapped in `@media (hover: hover)`, and `motion-safe:` and `starting:` variants exist (official-docs, https://tailwindcss.com/docs/hover-focus-and-other-states).
7. **The H1 never animates.** Chrome ignores paints at opacity 0 as LCP candidates, so a fading headline moves LCP to the end of the fade (official-docs, https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/refs/heads/main/docs/speed/metrics_changelog/lcp.md). **Correction:** DebugBear's fixes are to disable the fade or start it at 0.1; leaving only the LCP element out of a group animation is our inference (practitioner, https://www.debugbear.com/blog/opacity-animation-poor-lcp).

### Motion tokens

In `:root` of `app/globals.css`, beside the colour tokens:

```
--motion-tap: 120ms;      /* hover colour, focus ring, press */
--motion-enter: 200ms;    /* chevron, question-in, tooltips */
--motion-settle: 300ms;   /* sketch-in and the hero sketch; the page's hard cap */
--motion-stagger: 40ms;   /* per item, five items at most */
--ease-enter: cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1);
--ease-move: cubic-bezier(0.77, 0, 0.175, 1);
--ease-hover: ease;
```

In `@theme inline`: `--ease-out: var(--ease-enter)`, `--ease-in-out: var(--ease-move)`, `--ease-hover: var(--ease-hover)`, `--animate-question-in: question-in var(--motion-enter) var(--ease-enter) both`, `--animate-sketch-in: sketch-in var(--motion-settle) var(--ease-enter) both`. Tailwind 4's `--ease-*` and `--animate-*` namespaces turn these into `ease-enter`, `duration-(--motion-tap)` and `animate-sketch-in` with no dependency (official-docs, https://tailwindcss.com/docs/theme). May animate: opacity, `translate` up to 0.5 rem, scale 0.97 to 1, rotate on the FAQ chevron, colour, background-colour, border-colour. Never: width, height, padding, margin, inset, font-weight, letter-spacing, box-shadow, blur, the GlowBackdrop, the H1, anything on a loop. Replace the reduced-motion block: keep colour and opacity transitions at full length, put the `translate` in `question-in` and `sketch-in` inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` with a fade-only base, zero `--motion-stagger`, `scroll-behavior: smooth` under `motion-safe` only. The standards rule that tunable numbers live in `lib/config.ts` cannot be met by CSS; record `app/globals.css` as the home of motion tokens in an ADR.

Concrete applications: CTA press `motion-safe:active:scale-[0.97]` with `transition-[color,background-color,transform]`, hover darkens fill only, focus ring instant (INP: `:active` is pure CSS, no handler cost; official-docs, https://web.dev/articles/optimize-inp). FAQ answer fades in via `starting:opacity-0` (`@starting-style`, Baseline August 2024; official-docs, https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@starting-style); height is never animated. On `/start`, slide the next question from the right and a previous one from the left (origin-aware motion, Sonner's pattern; practitioner, https://sonner.emilkowal.ski/).

### Typography, because it is the page's image

Geist as served by `next/font/google` has a single `wght` axis and no `opsz`, so `font-optical-sizing` does nothing (official-docs, `node_modules/next/dist/compiled/@next/font/dist/google/font-data.json`; https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/font-optical-sizing). Optical sizing is manual: `tracking-tight` (not `tracking-tighter`, which the tell lists call crushed spacing) on h1 and h2, normal tracking on body, `text-pretty` on paragraphs, `text-balance` kept on headings (Chromium limits balance to six lines; official-docs, https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/text-wrap). Replace the three breakpoint steps with a fluid scale of six `--text-*` tokens, each `clamp()` carrying a rem term (for example `clamp(2.25rem, 1.4rem + 3.5vw, 4rem)` for the h1), then test every heading at 200% zoom on 1280 px and 1920 px: viewport-only sizing fails WCAG 1.4.4 (official-docs, https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Techniques/failures/F94.html; practitioner, https://utopia.fyi/blog/css-modular-scales). Measure 45 to 90 characters (practitioner, https://practicaltypography.com/line-length.html). One dark band only, the closing CTA, with an AA-checked `--on-scrim-muted` token and no glow on it; the glow tokens are cyan and blue, and cyan-on-dark is the most recognised tell. Do not cite the "whitespace raises comprehension 20% (Lin 2004)" figure anywhere; the author says the paper is unrelated (observed, https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/lin-2004-did-discover-margins-white-space-increase-20-carl-myhill).

## 5. GSAP and the animation architecture for this codebase

### Facts, corrected

- **Version and licence.** gsap 3.15.0 (13 April 2026) is the latest; the npm licence field is "Standard 'no charge' license" (official-docs, https://registry.npmjs.org/gsap/latest). Since 3.13 (29 April 2025) every plugin including ScrollTrigger, ScrollSmoother, SplitText, Flip and MorphSVG is free for commercial use (official-docs, https://gsap.com/blog/3-13/). **Correction:** the licence text contains no attribution requirement (it bars removing proprietary notices), and it never mentions open source or OSI; the field-of-use restriction (no use in tools that build visual animations without code and compete with Webflow) is what conflicts with clause 6 of the Open Source Definition, which is the verifier's inference (official-docs, https://gsap.com/standard-license/, https://gsap.com/licensing/, https://opensource.org/osd). A studio marketing site is a permitted use; the exposure is that the licence is revocable, unlike Motion's MIT.
- **The React hook.** `@gsap/react` 2.1.2's `useGSAP` is `useLayoutEffect` when `document` exists and `useEffect` otherwise, reverts a `gsap.context` on cleanup, and `contextSafe` wraps later-running functions so their tweens are reverted too (official-docs, https://gsap.com/resources/React/). Next's App Router has StrictMode on by default, which runs an extra setup and cleanup cycle per Effect in development (official-docs, https://react.dev/reference/react/StrictMode; https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/config/next-config-js/reactStrictMode); the hook's revert is what prevents doubled tweens.
- **Hydration.** **Correction:** ScrollTrigger's temporary `body.style.borderTopStyle = "solid"` write happens in `ScrollTrigger.enable()`, at registration, not in `refresh()`, and gsap 3.15.0 already resets the body `style` attribute afterwards with the source comment "SSR frameworks like Next.js complain if this attribute gets added" (source, `ScrollTrigger.js` lines 2085 to 2110 in 3.15.0). The Next 15 hydration thread is real and GSAP staff's advice was to run all GSAP code client-side only and use `useGSAP` (practitioner, https://gsap.com/community/forums/topic/43281-hydration-error-in-nextjs-15/). Verify in dev with StrictMode before shipping; loading after mount sidesteps it either way.
- **`gsap.from` on server markup.** The flash is real: HTML arrives first, the from-tween applies later, so the element shows its end state, jumps back, then animates. **Correction:** the thread's remedy is to set the hidden initial state in CSS and animate in a layout effect; it does not say entrances must not be JS-driven. That is our inference, and it holds for this page because CSS-hidden hero content is blank with JavaScript off, which the owner's degrade rule forbids (practitioner, https://gsap.com/community/forums/topic/35261-gsapfrom-not-working-correctly-in-next-js/).
- **Bytes.** Measured with esbuild (minify, ESM, React external, gzip 9 / brotli 11) on 3 September 2026: core 27.7 / 25.0 KB; core plus ScrollTrigger 45.3 / 40.7; plus SplitText 48.3 / 43.4; core plus useGSAP 28.1 / 25.5; the full set with Flip, ScrollSmoother and MorphSVG 71.7 / 63.4. Two verifiers reproduced every figure within 0.4 to 1.7 KB (evidence, reproduced). **Correction:** npmjs.com carries no size figures; cite the method. - **ScrollTrigger mechanics.** Pinning uses `position: fixed` only when the scroller is the body, transforms otherwise; any ancestor with `transform`, `filter`, `backdrop-filter`, `will-change` or `contain` re-parents fixed elements and breaks the pin; `pinSpacing` defaults to false inside `display: flex`; `content-visibility` breaks position calculation; create triggers in page order (official-docs, https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/). `refresh()` fires on `visibilitychange`, `DOMContentLoaded`, `load` and `resize` and must be called by hand after dynamic content or image loads; font swaps are our reasonable addition (official-docs, https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/static.config()/). **Correction:** `ScrollTrigger.batch()` still creates one ScrollTrigger per element; what it batches is the callbacks, delivering an array so one staggered tween runs instead of N (official-docs, https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/static.batch()/). `normalizeScroll` is opt-in and "experimental". `gsap.matchMedia` accepts `(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` as a condition and reverts everything created inside when it stops matching (official-docs, https://gsap.com/docs/v3/GSAP/gsap.matchMedia()/). `force3D: "auto"` uses `translate3d` during a tween and drops to 2D after. **Correction:** the CSS plugin docs say nothing about `will-change`; the only `will-change` write in the 3.15.0 package is in ScrollSmoother's `effects()`, and the same file carries the author's comment that `will-change: transform` "performed noticeably worse on iOS" (source, `ScrollSmoother.js`).
- **The native alternatives.** Next 16's App Router vendors a React canary (19.3.0-canary locally) that exports `ViewTransition` and `Activity` with no configuration; route navigations are transitions, so `<ViewTransition>` animates automatically and unsupported browsers simply do not animate (official-docs, https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/view-transitions). The installed `react` 19.2.8 does not export `ViewTransition` and `@types/react` 19.2.18 declares it only in `canary.d.ts`; **Unverified:** whether a `react/canary` types reference makes it typecheck here. `Activity` is stable in 19.2 and destroys Effects while hidden, so `useGSAP` inside an `Activity` subtree reverts its triggers automatically (official-docs, https://react.dev/blog/2025/10/01/react-19-2). CSS scroll-driven animations run on the compositor in Chrome and Edge 115+ and Safari 26; Firefox stable (155 on 1 September 2026) lacks them and caniuse's "Firefox 158" is a future train (official-docs, MDN browser-compat-data; evidence, https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_properties_animation-timeline). Next 16 targets Chrome, Edge and Firefox 111 and Safari 16.4, so every such effect must be additive (official-docs, https://nextjs.org/docs/architecture/supported-browsers). **Correction:** the lazy-loading guide's on-demand `import()` example runs in an event handler, not an effect; an effect works the same way (official-docs, https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/lazy-loading).

### The architecture

A three-tier policy, written into `docs/standards.md`:

- **Tier 0, CSS, everything on the page today.** Keyframes, `@starting-style`, `view()` timelines behind `@supports (animation-timeline: view())` under `motion-safe`, with the static state as the complete fallback. Extend `section-views.tsx`'s IntersectionObserver into a leaf that sets `data-inview` if a reveal is ever wanted (it is not recommended). Zero library bytes; the page is complete with JavaScript off.
- **Tier 1, React ViewTransition.** The home to `/start` hand-off (matching `name` on the hero sketch and the `/start` sketch, the hero `Link` prefetched so the pair forms in one commit, `transitionTypes` for forward and back) and the five question changes. Wrap `page.tsx`, not the layout. Copy the guide's two CSS rules into `globals.css`: `::view-transition { pointer-events: none }` and the reduced-motion block that zeroes `::view-transition-old(*)`, `::view-transition-new(*)` and `::view-transition-group(*)` durations. Vercel's Frames demo is the reference (observed, https://react-view-transitions-demo.labs.vercel.dev).
- **Tier 2, GSAP, one named effect only.** The examples slot, if it shows the demo sketch painting itself step by step (sentence, headline, logo, colour) once on enter. A static-screenshot gallery needs no library at all. Structure: `lib/motion/gsap.ts` (`'use client'`, imports `client-only`, registers ScrollTrigger and `useGSAP` once, re-exports); an ESLint `no-restricted-imports` rule so only `lib/motion` may import `gsap`; the gallery's client leaf awaits `import('@/lib/motion/gsap')` in an effect after an IntersectionObserver with a `rootMargin` says the section is near; the server renders the final markup so the box never changes size; tweens touch only `x`, `y`, `scale`, `rotation` and `autoAlpha`; every timeline is built inside `gsap.matchMedia({ ok: '(prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)', md: '(min-width: 768px)' })`; `ScrollTrigger.config({ ignoreMobileResize: true })`; `refresh()` after `document.fonts.ready` and after gallery images load; no pinning anywhere; no Tailwind `transition-*` or `will-change-*` on tweened elements (the sketch parts currently carry `transition-colors duration-400/500` and must lose them where GSAP drives them). Every duration, stagger and `rootMargin` in `CONFIG.motion`. Budget: no more than 30 KB gzipped added, no long animation frame over 50 ms in the first five seconds on the mid-tier preset (section 7).
- **Not adopted.** Motion alongside GSAP (two runtimes double the payload and split the reduced-motion logic; if a later product surface needs state-driven presence, use `LazyMotion` with `domAnimation` and `MotionConfig reducedMotion="user"`, and remove GSAP). ScrollSmoother and Lenis (a smoothing wrapper would restructure the root layout around a fixed header, `scroll-padding-top` anchors and `/#start` fragment links, for an effect NN/g's prospects would leave over). SplitText or TextPlugin on the H1 (it is the LCP text and the page's one sentence of trust). Flip where a ViewTransition can express the same morph. React Compiler is stable in Next 16 but off (`next.config.ts` has no `reactCompiler`); no compatibility reports for `useGSAP` were found either way, and `'use no memo'` is the documented escape hatch (official-docs, https://react.dev/reference/react-compiler/directives/use-no-memo).

### Degradation

JavaScript off: the complete page, the painted example sketch, static gallery images. Reduced motion: final states rendered instantly, colour and opacity transitions kept. Unsupported browser: no view transition, no scroll-driven timeline, same content. GSAP fails to load: the server-rendered final frame is what people see. A Playwright project asserts all four.

### Rules that follow from the metrics

- INP counts clicks, taps and key presses only, and a long task is anything over 50 ms (official-docs, https://web.dev/articles/inp, https://web.dev/articles/optimize-long-tasks). **Correction:** web.dev ties long tasks to input delay and large DOMs to presentation delay, not each to all three phases (official-docs, https://web.dev/articles/optimize-inp). Any keystroke handler on the sketch over 50 ms is split with a `scheduler.yield` helper (Chrome 129+, Firefox 142+) falling back to `setTimeout(0)` on Safari.
- Composited `translate` does not count toward CLS; `top` and `left` do even on their own layer; shifts within 500 ms of input are excluded, so the sketch's text changes while typing are free but a scroll reveal that changes height is not (official-docs, https://web.dev/articles/optimize-cls). Every gallery image gets width, height and `sizes`; without `sizes` the browser assumes 100vw (official-docs, image.md).
- Any above-the-fold image is the mobile LCP element: static import, `preload` on exactly one (the `priority` prop is deprecated in Next 16), `fetchPriority="high"` and `loading="eager"` on other first-screen images, never lazy (official-docs, image.md, https://web.dev/articles/optimize-lcp). Fetch priority moved Google Flights' LCP from 2.6 s to 1.9 s (evidence, https://web.dev/articles/fetch-priority). A white wireframe screenshot compresses below Chrome's 0.05 bits per pixel entropy threshold and is excluded from LCP, so a "good" LCP can hide a slow hero image (official-docs, https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/refs/heads/main/docs/speed/metrics_changelog/2023_04_lcp.md).
- `experimental.inlineCss` in production removes the one render-blocking stylesheet request (one 150 ms RTT on the profile) for a mostly first-time audience; it duplicates styles in the RSC payload, so measure HTML gzip before and after (official-docs, `inlineCss.md`). **Correction:** `experimental.turbopackRustReactCompiler` (16.3) selects the Rust implementation but does not turn the compiler on; it requires `reactCompiler: true` (official-docs, `turbopackRustReactCompiler.md`). `cacheComponents` adds nothing to a static page.

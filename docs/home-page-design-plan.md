# PinnaclePX home page: the design upgrade plan

Prepared 3 September 2026 from six research sweeps (studios, imagery, motion, GSAP, copy, performance; two of them re-checked by four independent verifiers) and eight expert critiques of the page as built (art director, motion designer, typographer, copywriter, conversion marketer, a sceptical business owner, a front-end engineer and a mobile reviewer), whose 123 proposals were cross-checked for feasibility and honesty. The research, with every corrected claim marked, is `docs/home-page-design-research.md`. This plan keeps the persuasion arc and the decisions in `docs/home-page-plan.md`; where it changes one, it says so. The owner's brief: keep the page's identity and take it to the next level, with imagery, motion and interaction, so that a visitor trusts the studio to build their site. Every number below is from the screenshots and the build measured on 3 September 2026.

## Built on 3 September 2026

Phases 0, 1a, 1b and 1c are implemented on `feat/home-page`. Phase 2 waits on the templates. Where the build departs from the text below, this is why:

- Section eyebrows (section 6) were not added: the design system's craft floor bans a kicker above a heading, so footer and nav labels resolve through the section headings alone.
- The How it works walkthrough switches its discrete stages with an IntersectionObserver rather than a scrubbed ScrollTrigger, so GSAP is loaded for the hero sequence only (ADR 0005). `@gsap/react` is not used because its hook imports GSAP statically, which would defeat the lazy load.
- The spacing token is `--spacing-column` (utilities `p-column`), not `--spacing-block`: Tailwind 4.3's `inline-*` utility for `inline-size` turned `inline-block` into a width once a spacing token named `block` existed.
- The initial JavaScript budget on `/` is 210 KB: the built page is 201 KB, of which about 155 KB is the React and Next floor; page-owned code is 46 KB against its 50 KB budget.
- The How it works beats carry no 01 to 03 numerals: "Question N of 5" on the frame already counts, and the taster's numbered steps sit directly beneath, so the finish review asked for one numbered list, not two. The footer link to the taster reads "What an hour does" so it resolves on arrival without an eyebrow.
- The owner chose not to run the Companies House check on the example names.
- Later still, at the owner's request, the hero's authored moment became a loop: after the brief completes, the wireframe builds into a finished page, holds, clears and starts again. The plan is `docs/hero-loop-plan.md` and the decision, which amends this plan's "never" list and ADR 0005 item 6, is ADR 0006. The example brief gained one photograph; the stylesheet budget below moved from 12 KB to 14 KB. The owner then had the Pause control removed as unwanted chrome, so the hero's "Pause" and "Replay" below, and the 2.2.2 line in section 8, no longer describe the build: the loop runs with no control and the page knowingly fails that criterion (ADR 0006, decision 2). Later still, section 3.2's phone strip below `lg` was replaced: below `md` the phone frame shows in full under the button, and from `md` the browser frame with the phone over its corner appears, narrower than on desktop for the LCP (`docs/hero-loop-mobile-plan.md`).
- Later the same day, at the owner's request: the example brief is VetPres (a software product most visitors recognise) with its own description and its teal typed as a custom hex; the sentence field sits above the primary button in the hero at every size, so question one is answered on the home page; the phone frame has a phone's proportions (9:19) with feature rows and a footer to fill it; the About craft line is gone; and the "first look, not the finished site, imagine an hour with us" message is written into the hero, the taster and the FAQ.
- The photograph plates, the town, the contact email and the JSON-LD address are wired to nullable fields in `lib/site.ts` and render nothing until the owner supplies them.
- The FAQ item about replacing an existing site does not promise the domain stays theirs; that clause waits for the owner's confirmation. "When do I pay, and do I own it?" waits for the owner's terms.

## 1. Where the page stands

The page is honest, fast and empty. It sells "see your new website" and shows no website, no process and no studio: the only picture on the whole page is a status card reading "Question 4 of 5" sitting alone in a column padded 64 px on every side, the hero at 1440x900 is 570 px of centred text over a glow, and on a phone the page is 6,001 px tall with one still card in seven screens. From the taster down, four consecutive sections share one composition (heading left, grey prose right), so strip the words and the section that books the call is the same shape as the one that says who we are. The persuasive sentences (what you get, straight answers, the taster steps, the FAQ) are the smallest running text on the page, 14 px muted, read on a phone. The heading sizes have no system, so the FAQ title is as tall as any heading below the hero (48 px, level with How it works and the closing) while the taster, the section that books the call, is a size smaller. There is no motion beyond a hover colour, and the one reduced-motion rule flattens even the harmless colour transitions. And there is one defect: the corner ticks poke 11 px past a phone's viewport, so the page scrolls sideways under the thumb.

None of that is a reason to redesign. The identity is real and every critic said so: the max-w-7xl hairline column with its section rules and corner ticks, the two radial glows bracketing the page, Geist, the restrained blue ramp with the first text-safe step at `--brand-deeper`, the two-button system (the gradient pill in the hero and closing, black for the call), the click trigger under the hero, How it works and closing buttons, native details for the FAQ, four small client leaves on a server-rendered page, 0 findings from the design detector, and copy that names the burned buyer's exact scar in eight words. The measured baseline to defend: 191 KB of gzipped JavaScript on `/` of which about 155 KB is the React and Next floor and about 32 KB is page code, 9 KB of CSS, one 29 KB font file, zero images, LCP is the H1 on desktop and the subhead on a phone.

The single biggest gap, named independently by all eight critics, is that the studio's one demonstrable mechanism, the live sketch on `/start` (a wireframe browser and phone frame that paint themselves from the visitor's answers, with a colour sweep that keeps the hue and moves only lightness and chroma), is hidden one click behind the button. The page asks for belief and offers no evidence. Everything else in this plan is rhythm, type, two honest photographs, and giving each section a silhouette of its own.

## 2. The design thesis

**The protagonist is the sketch.** It appears three times, at three sizes, as one thing seen from three distances: the browser frame with the phone over its corner in the hero, painting an example brief in the order the five questions ask; the phone frame alone in How it works, painted by the visitor's own scrolling; and the phone frame, small and still, in the closing section, showing what the visitor typed at the top if they typed anything. Top of the page is a browser, bottom is a phone, and the same answers are on both.

**The one authored moment is the colour sweep.** An example sentence types itself into a grey homepage, the company name lands as the headline, the wordmark and the tab title, the style fills the image block, and on the last beat the whole frame and the glow behind it turn from brand blue to that business's own colour in one transition. It is the studio's actual colour rule (`tintsFrom` in `lib/brief/sketch.ts`) running in front of the visitor, which is why a competitor cannot paste it: a static mock cannot turn your colour. It plays once, about six seconds, then hands over.

**The scroll is paced, not decorated.** Reading top to bottom the page alternates: artefact (hero), strip (what you get), artefact painted by scroll (how it works), band (examples, phase 2), photograph over numbered steps (taster), grid (straight answers), card and photograph (about), accordion (FAQ), glow with the phone frame (closing). No two adjacent sections share a shape. Three lists rise in as lists, once. Nothing else moves unless the visitor does something.

**What never happens on this page.** No preloader, no autoplay loop, no parallax, no marquee, no cursor follower, no reveal on every section, no pinned sections, no scroll snapping or hijacking, no hover lift with a shadow, no animated blur, no motion on the H1 or the hero subhead (one of them is the LCP element at every size), no SplitText on body text, no stock people, no client logos, no testimonials, no price, no founder face, no fake browser frame that is not the labelled sketch, and no word that claims the pipeline exists before it does.

## 5. Motion and interaction system

**The GSAP architecture.** GSAP 3.15.0 is free for commercial use under Webflow's standard no-charge licence, every plugin included; `@gsap/react` 2.1.2 supplies `useGSAP`, which reverts its context on unmount and survives StrictMode's double effects. It earns its place in exactly two leaves, the hero timeline (labels, pause, replay) and the How it works scrub, and it is never in the initial bundle:

- `lib/motion/gsap.ts`, marked `client-only`: one cached promise that dynamically imports `gsap` and `gsap/ScrollTrigger`, registers the plugin, calls `ScrollTrigger.config({ ignoreMobileResize: true })`, and resolves `gsap`. An ESLint `no-restricted-imports` rule allows only `lib/motion` to import `gsap`. Loaded after `requestIdleCallback` (with a `setTimeout` fallback) or the first scroll, whichever comes first, and only when `matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)')` matches, so it never competes with hydration or the LCP paint and reduced-motion visitors never download it.
- Every tween is built inside `gsap.matchMedia().add('(prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)', ...)` inside `useGSAP({ scope })`, so a preference change or an unmount reverts everything. `ScrollTrigger` conditions add `(min-width: 768px)` so phones never scrub.
- The server always renders the finished state. A leaf rewinds to the start only in its layout effect and only when motion is allowed; no `opacity-0` or `translate` class ever ships in server HTML. Tweens touch `x`, `y`, `scale`, `rotation` and `autoAlpha` only. No pinning anywhere (the sticky columns already give the pinned feel at zero cost and a pin escapes the bordered column); no `SplitText`; no `Flip` in phase 1. `refresh()` once after `document.fonts.ready`. The sketch parts drop their `transition-colors` classes only where GSAP drives the same property.
- Budget: the GSAP chunk under 50 KB gzipped (core plus ScrollTrigger measure 44.7 KB before any wrapper code), never referenced from the initial script tags of `/`, no long animation frame over 50 ms in the first five seconds on the mid-tier throttled preset.
- Not adopted: Motion alongside GSAP (two runtimes), ScrollSmoother or Lenis (a smoothing wrapper would restructure the root layout around the fixed header and the fragment links for an effect the research's evidence says visitors leave over), SplitText on the H1 or the subhead, GSAP for the list reveals (CSS does it), a GSAP hero on a page with the React Compiler off (`'use no memo'` is the documented escape hatch if it is ever turned on).

**Degradation, tested.** JavaScript off: the complete page, the painted example sketch, the closing frame, three stacked example cards in phase 2. Reduced motion: final states rendered instantly, colour and opacity transitions kept, no GSAP download. GSAP fails to load: the server-rendered final frame is what people see. A Playwright project asserts each (section 8).

## 8. Performance and accessibility budgets

Pass lines are Google's at the 75th percentile, split by mobile and desktop; the lab profile is Lighthouse's default mobile throttling (150 ms RTT, 1.6 Mbps down, 4x CPU, "roughly the bottom 25% of 4G"), which matches the audience: 71% of UK mobile connections were still 4G in Ofcom's latest data, and iOS is 51% of UK mobile, so Safari is a first-class target.

Rules that follow: the H1 and the hero subhead never animate and are never faded in from opacity 0 (the subhead is the LCP element at 390 px, the H1 at 1440; Chrome does not count an element painted at opacity 0 as an LCP candidate); the auto-updating sketch sizes its empty slots in line-height units (`min-h-[3lh]` for the paragraph, `min-h-[1lh]` for the headline, a fixed box for the feature mark) and sits inside a fixed-aspect stage so no internal change can move the caption or the section below; any keystroke handler on the sketch over 50 ms yields with `scheduler.yield` where it exists and `setTimeout(0)` on Safari; `content-visibility: auto` with `contain-intrinsic-size` on the FAQ and footer, never on a section that carries a ScrollTrigger; a Cal.com embed, if ever used, on `next/script` `lazyOnload`; `experimental.inlineCss` measured on the HTML gzip before adoption; a `WebVitals` leaf with `useReportWebVitals` beaconing LCP, INP and CLS with attribution, and a `long-animation-frame` observer in `instrumentation-client.ts` beaconing frames over 50 ms with their top script, because that is how a library's cost is measured rather than guessed.

CI gates, all required on pull requests into `main`:

1. `scripts/bundle-budget.mjs`, plain Node: after `next build`, parses the prerendered HTML for `/` and `/start`, gzips every referenced chunk and stylesheet, fails over budget, and fails if any chunk containing `gsap` is referenced from the initial script tags of `index.html`.
2. Lighthouse CI (`treosh/lighthouse-ci-action`) against the Vercel preview URL or `next start` on the runner, five runs, mobile and desktop presets, the assertions above at `error`, byte budgets as assertions.
3. ## 9. Business case and measurement

What the page is for has not changed: previews per 100 home sessions, which is start rate (`brief_step` 1 over sessions) times completion (`brief_step` 5 over `brief_step` 1) times pipeline success (`preview_ready` over `brief_step` 5); then calls per 100 previews (`call_booked` over `preview_ready`, which needs the real Cal.com webhook). Nothing can be measured until the pipeline exists, so the honest framing of this plan is: the page cannot convert today because there is no product behind the button; the upgrade's job is to make the first five seconds show the product working, so that when the pipeline lands the page is not the bottleneck. Every section change above maps to one ratio: the hero sketch, the trigger and the mobile header state move start rate; one question at a time, the sketch as reward and the hand-over field move completion; the taster bridge, the call agenda, the address card and a real booking link move calls.

Events to add (all typed in `lib/analytics/events.ts`): `design_open { index }` on the done-screen links, `contact_click`, `example_open { business }` (phase 2), `sketch_replay`, `sketch_pause`, `brief_focus { location }`, plus `what-you-get` and `cta` in `SECTION_IDS` so reaching the bottom is known. Server events with the pipeline: `lead_created`, `preview_ready { durationMs }`, `call_booked` from a signed Cal.com webhook. Vercel Web Analytics drops query strings, so per-question drop-off comes from `brief_step`, not `/start?q=N` page views. Custom events depend on the Vercel plan.

Tests, once traffic exists, run as sequential four-week blocks (one change at a time; a one-person studio's traffic will not power a split test until starts pass about 500 a month): 1, the hero with the example sketch versus a text hero, primary metric start rate, guardrails completion and mobile LCP; 2, the mobile header CTA state versus none, primary mobile start rate, guardrail completion (a persistent action can buy low-intent starts); 3, the H1 variant "See three homepage designs in your own brand before you hire anyone." (kept out of this plan so the OG image, the title and two e2e assertions change together only when a test can read the result). Tag the preview page's share action with `utm_source=share` so forwarded links are a separable source.

If time is short, cut in this order: phase 1c (the hand-over field and the closing frame's memory), the photograph plates (ship the sections without them), the header scrolled state, the FAQ height transition, the eyebrows. Never cut: the hero sketch with its label, the fold guard, the reduced-motion fix, the overflow defect, the type and spacing tokens, the CI gates.

## 11. Risks and open decisions for the owner

1. **The pipeline is still the blocker.** Nothing here makes the page a lead source; it makes the page ready for one. Recommendation: build phase 0 and 1a now, in parallel with the pipeline, and keep the "no traffic to `/start`" rule.
2. **A wireframe sketch in the hero versus the plan's "no mock browser frames".** The earlier plan forbade mock frames because a fake preview reads as a lie. The sketch is not a preview: it is the mechanism, labelled at every appearance. Recommendation: allow it, with the label wording in section 4 fixed and never shortened.
3. **The example business names.** Go Wild Dog Walking, Mvmnt and VetPres must not collide with a real UK trader before one appears on the home page. Recommendation: Companies House and a web search this week; rename any that collide.
4. **Fixed quote on the call.** The taster's step 03, the call agenda and `callPromise` must agree. Recommendation: if a quote can be given inside every 20-minute call, say so in the agenda; if not, "a timeline on the call and a fixed quote in writing within one working day".
5. **Payment, ownership, domain.** The page names the deal that burned the visitor and never states this studio's deal. Recommendation: one plain FAQ answer with the true terms ("Nothing until you have seen your designs and agreed a fixed quote. The site, the domain and the words are yours."), only once every clause is true.
6. **One person or "the people".** Recommendation: "the person who will design and build your site". It is the differentiator, and it is true.
7. **"Wording" or "copy".** Recommendation: "wording" for the visitor, "copy" in code. Whichever wins, never mix them.
8. **GSAP.** It is free for this use, but the licence is revocable, unlike Motion's MIT, and it costs about 45 KB lazily, budgeted at 50. Recommendation: adopt it for the two leaves named, record ADR 0005, and revisit only if the field data shows long frames on phones.
9. **The React `ViewTransition` hand-off.** The installed `react` 19.2.8 does not export it; Next's vendored canary does. `@types/react` declares it only in `canary.d.ts`, so `tsconfig` `types` needs `"react/canary"` before `tsc` accepts the import (verified 3 September 2026). Recommendation: build it last in phase 1c, add the types reference, and drop it without regret if it misbehaves.
10. **The Vercel plan.** Hobby is non-commercial and caps image transformations; Speed Insights breakdowns need Pro. Recommendation: Pro before the page takes traffic.
11. **Templates and container queries.** Must be decided before t01 is written or the examples band cannot show a template at frame size. Recommendation: container queries, with the CI grep.
12. **The hero hand-over field on phones.** The fold cannot hold a field, a sketch strip and the button at 390x844. Recommendation: desktop only in phase 1c; on phones the CTA opens `/start`, where question one is the whole screen.
13. **The H1.** Every critic's keep list has it; the copywriter and the research would change it now. Recommendation: keep it, fix the walk-back in the subhead, and run it as test 3.
14. **Photographs of real businesses.** They may carry the "no people" rule and must never sit under an invented business name. Recommendation: the shot list and caption rule in section 4, and the owner confirms the no-people rule covers supplied images.
15. **Follow-up for people who reach a preview and never book.** One reminder email is defensible under "Nobody calls you unless you book"; more breaks the promise. Recommendation: one email, stated in the privacy notice.
16. **Google reviews.** A Google Business Profile with real reviews is verifiable third-party credibility, not a testimonial. Recommendation: allowed once real reviews exist; never quoted on the page as testimonials.
17. **"AI" once.** The retained question "Is this AI?" plus one mention in its answer puts the word on the page twice. Recommendation: accept the question as the one place the rule tolerates; if the rule is strict, ask "Is a computer doing this?" instead.

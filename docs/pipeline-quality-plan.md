## 1. Every model call

The frame: the Inngest function `build-concepts` (`lib/inngest/functions/build-concepts.ts`) runs select, tokens, brief, then copy and imagery in parallel; `sweep-deadline` writes the fallback into anything still open 45 s before the five-minute deadline (`CONFIG.deadline`). A failed step is retried every 20 s, up to 14 times (`CONFIG.pipeline`). Every call ends in `noteModelCall` (`lib/ai/usage.ts`): an `ai.call` log line and a `model_call` row, the row's failure swallowed as `ai.call.unrecorded`; the row carries no attempt number and no violation count. The one SDK client (`lib/ai/client.ts`) keeps the SDK's default of two internal retries on 408, 409, 429, 5xx, connection errors and timeouts, so a stage's timeout is a per-request ceiling: the brief's 20 s can become 60 s and the copy's 60 s can become 180 s before Inngest sees a failure.

## 5. Levers, in the cost guide's order, each with its ceiling

Ceilings are estimated per submission against the 7.1-cent typical case. Ranked by ceiling: 7 and 8 (runaway retries), 6 (first-answer fit), 10 (slot-only retry), the quality fixes 2, 9 and 13 (no token ceiling, pages better), then 3 to 5 (input hygiene), 11 (rank size), 1 (caching, nil).

**Free wins** (no quality cost expected; a prompt change still goes through the eval; anything under `templates/` passes the two grep guards)

1. **Caching: nothing to gain today.** Checked: no request carries a marker; `copyPrompt` puts the volatile brief first and the stable guide last, so the only prefix every call shares is the 202-token system prompt, under Sonnet 5's 1,024 minimum. A marker at the end of the first user turn would serve the in-call retry and the 20-second step re-attempt on all eight templates (the whole first prompt is 1,242 to 2,123 tokens); guide first would also serve a same-template submission within five minutes, on six of eight templates (Aurora and Vector fall short unless the schema counts in the prefix, which is unmeasured). Either pays only when more than 28% of calls are followed by a read (write 1.25×, read 0.1×), and the pass aims at no retries. Leave alone; revisit at volume. Ceiling: nil.
2. **Input, quality: the owner's sentence is missing from the copy call.** Checked: `copyPrompt` says "use the brief's own words and the owner's sentence"; `ownersWords` reaches only `ruleViolationsIn`. Sending it costs up to 111 tokens a call ($0.0007 a submission) and lets the model quote what the rules allow; today the system prompt forbids every number and superlative outright, so the prompt needs the "unless the owner said it" exception the code already grants. A fit lever too: a brief that paraphrases "since 2009" into "fifteen years" invites "15", a violation.
3. **Input: the guide's boilerplate.** Checked over all eight guides (34,678 characters): 55% is purpose text the schema cannot carry (counts, order, "the same as ctaLabel", examples) and must stay; the strict redundancy is 30 purposes that only restate the path, 20 sibling pairs that repeat a count, and 9 enum tails the schema's description already lists: 6.3% of the guide, about 70 tokens a call. The per-line " characters" is another 11.3%, stated once in the system prompt already. Together about 190 tokens a call, $0.001 a submission. Small money, a clearer prompt.
4. **Input: brief fields the copy call does not need.** Checked: `imageQueries` goes to every copy call and only `planImagery` reads it: 152 characters, about 40 tokens, and search phrases the model is told not to introduce. `tone` and `headlines[1..2]` are read by no code but are context for the model, so they stay. Negligible money; do it with lever 3.
5. **Input: redundant system lines.** "Answer only with JSON in the schema you are given" is enforced by `output_config.format`; "Second person" fits four of the brief's ten fields and fights its first-person-plural statement; "Do not name a place" fights the image queries that must name one. About 40 tokens of a 202-token prompt; a brief-specific system prompt removes the three contradictions.
6. **Loop: first-answer fit.** Unmeasured: no observation of a violation rate exists anywhere in the repo, and the `model_call` row records neither the attempt nor the count. Checked, as the rules stand: a company name with a digit or Premier, Finest, Best or A1 is flagged in every slot that carries it unless the sentence happens to repeat it, so the ladder runs to the end and ships the fallback with the same name; any digit (`24/7`, `9 to 5`, a postcode, `£50`), `guarantee` inside "no guarantees", "Gas Safe certified" and a "Testimonials" heading are flagged; the substring allow-list passes `best` beside `asbestos` and `20` beside `2009`; the slot check counts UTF-16 units after a trim (an emoji is two); and Harbor's guide asks for one to three About heading lines while its validator demands two to three (`templates/t06-harbor/contract.ts:145` against `copy-slots.ts:274`), so a legal one-line heading is a count violation. Fixes in order: the company name joins the allow-list; the Harbor count agrees with its guide; matches are counted once a slot. Ceiling: 2.1 cents per avoided retry on a mean template, and every avoided retry is a fallback that never happens.
7. **Loop: copy calls whose result cannot reach the page.** Checked: `copy-start` is a memoised step, so a retried `copy-<id>` step never re-checks whether the sweeper settled the stage; attempts that start after 255 s still call the model, and only `copy-finish` notices. The brief and imagery stages re-check on every attempt through `runStage`. One row read before the call ends it. Ceiling: up to two paid calls a template per sweep.
8. **Loop: the API-failure path has no cap, and the SDK retries inside it.** Checked: `CONFIG.copy.attempts` bounds only the violations branch; a 429, 5xx or timeout retries the copy step on the 20-second cadence up to 15 attempts, each a fresh `writeCopy` of up to two calls (30 a template), and a first answer that merely broke a limit is thrown away when its in-call retry fails transiently. The client's `maxRetries` 2 means one `parse` can run three 60-second requests. Bound the failure branch by attempts or time, keep a judged first answer across attempts, and set `maxRetries` explicitly. This changes retry behaviour ADR 0015 and ADR 0017 record: proposed, not applied. Ceiling: the whole runaway, over $0.40 a submission.
9. **Output, quality: the guide and the retry name slots the JSON does not have.** Checked: Aurora's guide and violation paths are content-object paths (`statement.text`, `cta.action.label`, `hero.primary.label`, `nav.links[2].label`) while the schema asks for `statement`, `cta.action`, `hero.primary` and `nav.links` as strings; 56 of 356 guide paths differ from their JSON keys across the eight templates, because violations are computed over the assembled content. The model must map them on every call and on every retry. Report violations and write guides under the JSON keys. Ceiling: unmeasured fit gain; no token cost.
10. **Output: the brief's second image query and the echoed company.** Checked: `company` is overwritten after the call (about 7 output tokens); the second query is tried only when the first search returns nothing. Leave alone: lever 13 wants the second query.

**Tradeoffs** (only against the eval, and only with the owner's yes)

11. **Output: the retry regenerates the whole JSON.** A slot-only retry merged in code would cut a retry's output from about 1,200 tokens to about 100: 1.1 cents a retry. ADR 0011 decision 1 says the whole answer comes back; changing that is the owner's.
12. **Rank images: `medium` to `tiny` or `small`.** 2,656 fewer tokens a call at `small` ($0.005 a submission, 7% of the total), 2,185 at `tiny`. Whether a 130 or 200 px thumbnail still shows a watermark or text is unmeasured: the eval compares verdicts at both sizes on the same candidates. Fewer candidates is the wrong direction (lever 13). `docs/pipeline-plan.md` section 13 says `src.small`; the code sends `medium`.
13. **Quality: the detail picture pool is too small, and the verdict can overflow.** Checked: one detail search of 12 feeds every detail slot of all three templates; Ember needs 11 and Summit 12, so in 37 of 56 template sets the demand exceeds 12 before any rejection, pictures repeat across designs, and a rejected candidate leaves a slot null with no retry (`fill` returns null; only a thrown error is retried). A second detail search with the brief's second query, or a larger page, costs one more search and one more rank call ($0.0045) a submission: ADR 0012 decision 2 and ADR 0017 decision 3 territory. Cap the `reject` reason in the prompt, or make it an enum of the five faults, so 1,000 tokens is provably enough.
14. **Effort and thinking.** Thinking is disabled because thinking tokens were most of the copy bill (ADR 0011, amended; the 2,868-token call above). Adaptive thinking at `low` effort might fix more first answers for a few hundred output tokens each: a fit-rate trade to measure, never to assume.
15. **Model.** `claude-sonnet-5-5` costs the same and rejects `thinking: disabled` (it takes `between_tools` at effort `high` or below); Haiku 4.5 for copy would halve the copy bill with an unknown quality loss; Opus 5.5 doubles it. Any change is ADR 0009 decision 8: proposed with the eval's numbers, not applied.
16. **Batch API:** no use inside a five-minute deadline. Nothing to do.

## 7. The quality rubric (phase 2, for the owner's approval)

Every answer the harness stores is scored by code; twelve are read by hand; three rendered pages are the owner's. Each hand-read criterion scores 0 (fails), 1 (mostly) or 2 (clean), per template answer.

1. **No invented facts.** Every number, place, product, service, qualification, award, client or guarantee in the copy is in the owner's sentence or the company name. Code: `ruleViolationsIn` over the final copy (digits, superlatives, claims not in the sentence) and over the brief. Hand: anything the rules cannot catch (a street the owner did not name, a service they do not offer).
2. **British English in the owner's voice.** Code: a list of unambiguous American spellings. Hand: second person to the customer, first person plural for the business, contractions a tradesperson would use, no brochure register.
3. **Specific to the business, not agency filler.** Code: a filler list (elevate, seamless, journey, solutions, passionate, tailored, unlock, empower, leverage, cutting-edge, best-in-class, innovative, transform). Hand: would the owner recognise their trade in the headline, the three features and the steps, or could the page be any business?
4. **Fit on the first answer.** Code only: the share of template answers whose first call passed every slot limit, count and rule, by template and by slot; the share rescued by the in-call retry; the fallback rate.
5. **Pictures.** Code only: the share of candidates rejected and for which stated reason; verdicts naming no candidate; slots the stage would leave empty or fill with a picture another design took. Hand, on the three rendered pages: are the rejections right, and would the first-ranked picture sit on that business's homepage?
6. **Fallback rate.** Code only: template answers that ended in `fallbackCopy`, with the reason.

The twelve hand-read answers are fixed before reading: for each ready template, the first two fixtures in `tests/fixtures/eval/fixtures.json` order that chose it, until twelve are listed, so every template appears at least once and the three largest twice.

## 8. Baseline, measured (run `baseline`, 1 October 2026, before any lever)

Twenty fixtures, every stage, 99 model calls. The whole pass cost $1.12; a submission cost $0.056 on average ($0.017 to $0.119). That figure is not the pipeline's true cost: 36 of the 60 template answers made no call at all, because the API refused the copy call for Monolith, Meridian, Atlas, Harbor and Summit with a permanent 400, "The compiled grammar is too large", and the fallback copy stood at once. Only Aurora, Vector and Ember were written by the model.

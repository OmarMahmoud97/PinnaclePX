# Pipeline quality and token pass: the plan and its results

**For:** the owner. **From:** the pipeline pass, 1 October 2026. **Where:** worktree `../PinnaclePX-pipeline`, branch `feat/pipeline-quality` from `origin/main` at 5cae671, eight commits, unpushed. **Record:** ADR 0044. **Status:** phases 1 and 2 done and measured; phase 3 done for the deterministic defects, with the eval re-run and every prompt-wording lever waiting on credit; phase 4's real run waits on credit and the owner's word. Every number below says measured or estimated.

## 0. Summary for the owner

1. **The copy stage was broken for five of the eight templates.** The API refused the structured-output call for Monolith, Meridian, Atlas, Harbor and Summit with a permanent 400, "The compiled grammar is too large", so those templates always shipped fallback copy: 36 of 60 template answers in the baseline made no call. Nothing had ever measured a model call, so nobody saw it. The copy call now sends the shape as a JSON skeleton and checks the text with the schema; every template answers (commit ceff4f3).
2. **Measured cost of a completed submission, after that fix: $0.160 on average** over the thirteen fixtures whose three templates all answered, from $0.105 to $0.445. Copy is $0.137 of it, the brief $0.0084, the rank $0.0098. A clean first copy answer costs $0.0195; retries lift the mean to $0.0458 an answer. The $0.445 was the A1 Gas fixture, whose name broke the number rule on every attempt: fixed in b28dca2, so the mean of the other twelve, $0.136, is the better guide. The phase 1 estimate of 7 cents was wrong on two counts: it priced the broken stage's silence, and output tokens are larger than the slot ranges suggested.
3. **Six more defects fixed, each its own commit with tests:** copy calls after the sweeper had settled the stage, the SDK's two hidden retries inside a stage's budget, the company name outside the allow-list, rank verdicts matched on eight-digit ids with unjudged pictures accepted, a detail picture pool too small for Ember and Summit (63 empty and 106 repeated slots over twenty submissions), and Harbor's one-line About heading.
4. **The account ran out of credit twice.** Before the pass every call returned "credit balance is too low"; after the owner's top-up the runs spent $3.16 at the skill's prices (657,641 input and 204,386 output tokens) and the API refused again at 11:28 UTC, including the free token count. So the fixes after the skeleton are unit-tested but not re-measured, and the real end-to-end run has not happened. If production shares this key, live submissions are falling back right now.
5. **What a first answer gets wrong, measured:** one in two first copy answers fits (20 of the 42 that were made); the in-call retry fixed 25 of 37; 9 needed a fresh start; 3 fell back. Violations were 40 lengths, 45 rules (38 of them the digit in "A1"), 8 missing sections, 0 counts. Filler words 0, American spellings 0, broken emphasis 0 of 114. The brief carried an invented superlative or claim in 2 of 20.
6. **Pictures, measured:** the judge rejected 34% of candidates (faces 76 of 162), named every candidate correctly, and would have left 63 slots empty across the twenty fixtures. The detail pool now takes both detail queries; its effect is unmeasured until credit returns.
7. **Decisions for the owner** in section 6: credit first, then the re-run, the rubric, and the prompt-wording levers that wait for the eval.

## 1. Every model call, as the code now stands

The frame: `build-concepts` runs select, tokens, brief, then copy and imagery in parallel; `sweep-deadline` writes the fallback into anything still open 45 s before the five-minute deadline; a failed step is retried every 20 s, up to 14 times. Every call ends in `noteModelCall`: an `ai.call` log line and a `model_call` row (no attempt number, no violation count). The SDK client retries nothing itself (`CONFIG.ai.maxRetries`), so a stage's timeout is a ceiling.

## 7. The quality rubric (for the owner's approval)

Every answer is scored by code; twelve are read by hand; three rendered pages are the owner's. Hand-read criteria score 0, 1 or 2 per template answer.

1. **No invented facts.** Every number, place, product, service, qualification, award, client or guarantee is in the owner's sentence or the company name. Code: `ruleViolationsIn` over the final copy and the brief. Hand: what the rules cannot catch.
2. **British English in the owner's voice.** Code: a list of unambiguous American spellings. Hand: second person to the customer, first person plural for the business, no brochure register.
3. **Specific to the business, not agency filler.** Code: a filler list (elevate, seamless, journey, solutions, passionate, tailored, unlock, empower, leverage, cutting-edge, best-in-class, innovative, transform). Hand: would the owner recognise their trade in the headline, the features and the steps?
4. **Fit on the first answer.** Code only: first answers that passed every limit, by template and slot; retries that fitted; fallbacks.
5. **Pictures.** Code only: rejections and their stated reasons; verdicts naming no candidate; empty and repeated slots. Hand, on the three rendered pages: are the rejections right, and would the first-ranked picture sit on that homepage?
6. **Fallback rate.** Code only.

The twelve hand-read answers are the first fixtures in `fixtures.json` order that cover every template: joinery, A1 Gas, the dentist and the vague sentence, all three templates each. Their screenshots are under `test-results/eval/l0-skeleton/shots`. A provisional read of four pages: the Aurora dentist page is specific, British and true to the sentence ("0% finance on treatment over five hundred pounds", "since 1998"); the Meridian page for the vague sentence invents nothing and stays plain; the Ember and Summit pages depend on pictures; the Atlas page for A1 Gas shows the fallback that b28dca2 removes.

## 9. How to run the next pass

`pnpm eval` with `EVAL_RUN=<name>` runs every stage; `EVAL_STAGES=copy EVAL_REUSE_RUN=l0-skeleton` re-runs copy on the same briefs; `EVAL_STAGES=rank` the same for pictures; `EVAL_SUMMARISE=<run>` recomputes a summary; `EVAL_PLAN=1` checks the fixtures without a call. `pnpm dev -p 3100` then `pnpm eval:shots <run> http://localhost:3100 joinery,a1-gas,dentist-claims,vague` takes the twelve pages' screenshots. Spend per full pass, measured: about $2.50 with today's retry rate.
